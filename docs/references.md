# References

A reference is what a text cites, read by one parser from the text itself, never from a declared list.

Every text Seon reads as prose (a task's plan, a wiki page, a docstring) goes through ```seon.ref/parse```, and what it cites (```seon.ref/references``` keeps each once, and only what names something on the db) becomes that text's references: a plan's are its task's, a section's are its own, a docstring's are its definition's. The context pulls each one at the depth its citation asks for. The rule is v1's (`~/src/seon/docs/seon/schedule/cards/TASK-CHAIN.md` §3a), ported onto next's program facts.

## One parse

```seon.ref/parse``` reads Markdown once (given its path, or nil for a plan or a docstring) and returns its sections and its citations. A section has an address `path#anchor` (GitHub anchors), a heading, a level, a line span, a parent, a lead (the heading's next block when it is a paragraph) or an opening (any other first block), and its own text (the lines after its heading up to the next heading). A citation is a name, or a path with an optional anchor, with its depth and line, one per mention. ```seon.ref/scan``` is the scanner under it, one pass over the characters: ATX headings, fenced and indented code, code spans by CommonMark's equal-length backtick rule, inline links, and paragraphs ended by blank lines. A fenced code block carries its content's offsets (`:seon.ref/text-from`, `:seon.ref/text-to`, between its opening and closing lines) and a list block its items' (`:seon.ref/items`, each item's content after its marker, and `:seon.ref/ordered`), so a renderer cuts them with `subs`, never by a regex over the block. Setext headings, reference links and HTML blocks are out of scope; the subset was proven against 70 examples of the CommonMark 0.31.2 spec (`seon.ref-test/commonmark-examples-for-the-subset`). No Markdown library on Jolt gives a tree, which is why the scanner exists.

## The delimiter picks the depth

The delimiter around a citation decides how much of what it cites is pulled.

- A bare word holding a `/` is its **summary**, and counts only when it names something. A var's `#'` before it is punctuation, so `#'seon.context/printed-run` cites the var. A word read inside a `"..."` string is data, as quoted output is, never a citation: a reproduction's `"docs/flow.md"` names no page (`seon.ref-test/a-string-literal-in-prose-cites-nothing`). A code span between quotes is still a citation.
- One backtick, or a link, is its **doc**.
- Three or more backticks are its **code**.
- Two backticks are a literal, never a reference: write ``ns/name`` to mention a name without citing it.

Only a span of exactly one qualified symbol or keyword is a name. A form, an alias form such as `str/join`, a name followed by more words, and a name ending in `*` are code or prose. A span whose first segment is a top-level entry of the checkout is a path. A `path:N` or `path:A-B` citation is that file, marked `:line-reference`: line numbers go stale, so cite the var instead. ```seon.ref/cited``` keeps each name once, at its strongest depth, where it is first mentioned.

## Resolving and showing

```seon.ref/references``` resolves names against the program's `:var/name` facts, keywords against the schema registry and paths against the checkout; ```seon.ref/names``` is the qualified symbols alone (what the context uses; `seon.reconcile/hairy` reads the plan's cites instead, so a turn's merge check never runs a parser the agent changed: [failure points](failure-points.md#entrant-boundaries-a-defect-becomes-a-recorded-error-map)). ```seon.ref/view``` shows a section at a depth: at summary its heading and its lead's first sentence, at doc its lead and the headings below, at code the whole section. A section with no lead answers `:no-lead` at summary and doc depth, so every section should open with one sentence that stands alone.

A var is shown by the same depths (```seon.doc/resolve-ref```): at summary its arglists and its docstring's first sentence, at doc its arglists, docstring and contract, at code its recorded source and `:change`, the form that changes part of it (```seon.repl/edit!```, [changing a definition](agent.md#changing-a-definition)). The source is whole: the one printer cuts it where it is shown, breadth first under the context's budget, so every cited var shows before any one source is read far, and the cut reads its rest through the form that answers the task's data as data (`(subs (get-in (my.task/data) [:write-set 0 :source]) n)`, ```my.task/data```); a var with no docstring shows its source at doc depth. (It was swapped for the doc past `:seon.config.render/reference-tokens`, a cut before the printer, until the clipping audit of 2026-10-25; whole sources had been 82-88% of a measured task's context with no budget at all.)

A namespace at code depth is its ns form as recorded, so its requires and aliases, then a line for each definition, privates included, in file order (```seon.doc/namespace-source```); `(source ns)` shows the same text, so a citation and the REPL give one answer. At summary and doc depth it is the docstring and the definitions as before ([files and namespaces on demand](#files-and-namespaces-on-demand)).

A schema key a cite names joins its schema entry (`:schema/key`): its part shows at summary the form that reads it, at doc and code its form as written and `:change`, how the key changes at the REPL (``(my.schema/register! k form)``, starting from ``(get (my.schema/forms) k)``), never the resource file it merges into (an agent has no checkout). A key no schema registers is not nil: ``(my.schema/forms k)`` answers `:seon.schema/unknown-key` naming the keys its namespace registers, and a registration whose form is no EDN is refused naming the value and where it is (```seon.program/non-edn```).

## Cost

One parse is linear in the text, about 70 bytes allocated per byte of it (the 37 pages of the wiki, 0.9 MB: 54 MB, of which the scan 26 MB; 2026-10-29). A candidate name is read by its characters (```seon.ref/qualified-name```; `seon.ref-test/a-name-is-read-as-the-reader-reads-it` holds it equal to the reader), where `seon.reader/read-text` took about 13 µs and 18 KB a name, half of it `seon.reader/opts` rebuilt per call, and 3,328 spans of the wiki reach it: edamame on Jolt allocates about 2 KB per symbol it reads, so a read per span was most of a parse. The proof is `seon.ref-test/one-parse-costs-one-pass`.

## Task references

A task's cites are what its plan and instructions cite, written as cite entities by the write of the text and never authored.

```seon.task/create-tx```, ```seon.task/load-tx``` (a declaration changed) and ```seon.task/rederive-tx``` (any write an agent makes of its plan or instructions: a `swap!` of ctx, `my.task/update!`, its own `transact!`, all through `seon.agent/transact!`) parse the text once (```seon.ref/parse```), keep each target once at its strongest depth (```seon.ref/cited```) and the references the db holds (```seon.ref/kept```: a prose word only when it names something; a line reference and an anchor with no page name nothing to pull), and write them with ```seon.task/cites-tx```, the one writer, in the same transaction as the text: so no db holds the new text with the old cites, and no draw parses the plan with the program the agent may be changing (a parser the agent redefined, run by the layout each frame, would leave the page with no status). `seon.task-test/the-writer-writes-a-tasks-cites`, `a-rewritten-text-leaves-exactly-its-cites`; `seon.context-test/a-draw-reads-the-cites-its-plan-write-derived`.

A citation that resolves to nothing is a problem on the task, never an error (v1's rule, TASK-CHAIN §3): ```seon.task/citation-problems``` answers `{:task id :name sym :problem :unresolved}` for each, and `my.root/create!` and `my.root/load-tasks!` carry them as `:problems` beside the write, so whoever wrote the plan sees it when they write it. On the page the same cite joins nothing and its part shows `:unresolved true` with `:problem`, the message from ```seon.ref/problem-messages```: it resolves to nothing on the branch, what the task intends is its plan's to say, and two backticks mention a name without citing it. A plan that cites the function its proof calls, not the task's own name, is the cheapest way to a good context.

The program's vars among a task's cites (```seon.program/program-names```: a namespace the program holds) are what its judge reads (```seon.judge/subject```'s `:cited`) and what `:no-references` asks for (```seon.problems/uncited-tasks```). A reproduction's prose names a test helper and a handle (`result/e1`) that name nothing, so they are no cites (`seon.task-test/the-writer-cites-what-names-something`). A test the plan names is its proof (`:seon.task/proofs`), shown with its status, and the page leaves its cite out.

## An entity cites

A citation is an entity: `:seon.cite/of` the citing entity (a task or an agent), what it names, its depth and its place, drawn by its schema's two renders from what ctx joined for it.

- Its attributes (``:seon.cite/cite-fact``): `:seon.cite/address`, a section's `path#anchor`, or `:seon.cite/name`, a var, a schema key, a namespace or a task, a task's also its id as `:seon.cite/task` (a task's id is a string, a name a symbol: the pattern joins the task by it, and its part shows its plan, as ```seon.ref/task-view``` does; `seon.context-test/a-cited-task-shows-its-plan-at-that-depth`; the text is read on the db its write makes, so a task the same write declares is one); `:seon.cite/depth` (`:summary`, `:doc`, `:code`); `:seon.cite/order`, its first mention's place; and `:seon.cite/id`, its content id `"<of> <target>"`, by which a write upserts it. A page cited whole is written as its top section's address (```seon.ref/page-top```), its sections joined below it; a page or file no section of the db names keeps its address as written and joins nothing (a file is no longer read into the page: `(slurp path)` reads it).
- An address names its depth in its query, `path?depth#anchor` (`summary`, `lead`, `full`: today's `:summary`, `:doc`, `:code`), whatever its delimiter (```seon.ref/address-citation```).
- The pull joins them: the task-fact's and the agent-fact's `:cites`, and each need's (one level) ([the pattern](ctx.md#the-pattern)). The page's layout places one part per target, the task's, then its needs', then the agent's, each once at the strongest depth any asks, the task's proofs left out, in the order of what each builds on (a section after those its own `:seon.doc/references` reach), and fills a stub from where ctx holds the entity whole (an entity is held once). Each part is ```seon.render.ui/cited```, which reads ctx alone; its data is drawn by ```seon.render.ui/cite``` for the page and ```seon.render.ui/cite-model``` for the model ([a part answers data](render.md#a-part-answers-data)), and ```my.agent/doc``` of a name draws a cite of it made on demand by the same render, so on demand and on the page are one render.
- An agent is made citing what every agent cites, ``:seon.config.agent/cites``, written on its record by ```seon.task/agent-cites-tx``` when it begins its task on its branch (```seon.agent/begin-run!```: on main, a later merge of main would bring back a default it uncited; never read as a default at draw time), and changes its own: ```my.agent/cite!``` adds or deepens one, ```my.agent/uncite!``` retracts one (`seon.agent-test/an-agent-cites-what-it-reads-beside-its-task`, `seon.task-test/an-agent-is-made-citing-what-every-agent-cites`).
- A section main changed reaches ctx by the merge's datoms and re-sends its part alone (`seon.context-test/a-cited-section-main-changed-is-its-part-alone`).
- What a cite's var part shows of its tests and callers is the newest ``:seon.config.ctx/callers`` callers its pattern pulled, each with the vars it defines; past that window the part names ``(my.agent/tests-reaching 'v)``.

## The wiki in the store

The wiki's sections are entities on main, so who cites a section or a var is a database query.

```seon.doc/load-tx``` reads the pages it is given with the one parse and makes the db hold one entity per heading of each, leaving every other page as it is, `:seon.doc/fact`: its address (the parse's `path#anchor`, so ``docs/flow.md#idioms``), heading, lead, own text (the lines after its heading up to the next heading of any level) and parent. Its `:seon.doc/names` are the qualified names its text cites that name something at load (a prose word only when it names something, a backticked name always; ```seon.doc/load-tx``` through `seon.ref/kept`), and its `:seon.doc/read` the form id of each such var when the text was written ([health](health.md#a-stale-section)). Its `:seon.doc/references` are what its own text cites, resolved on the db: a link, or a cited `path#anchor`, reaches a section (a link with no anchor reaches the page's first section), and a name reaches its var by `:var/name`. A section's backlinks are therefore a query, the sections whose `:seon.doc/references` hold it (`seon.doc-test/backlinks-are-a-sections-instances`): a pattern's home knows its instances with no key of its own. A var's home is the backlink citing it at code depth, read from that section's own text by the same parse, so no depth is stored. ```seon.doc/load-dir-tx``` is the one load of `docs/`, and the one that retracts the sections of a page gone from the checkout (```seon.doc/load-tx``` of one page leaves the others as they are): root's boot writes it in the boot fact's commit (`seon.root/wiki-tx`, [root](root.md#boot)), and `my.root/load-docs!` writes it on demand.

Loading writes only what differs, as task files do (```seon.task/load-tx```): each section's stated attributes are made exactly what its text says by the one `seon.task/entity-tx` (a value main holds that the text dropped is retracted, one it lacks asserted), a section no page holds any more is retracted, and an unchanged wiki answers `[]`. A changed section rewrites only itself, because nothing stored depends on line numbers. A link, or a cited `path#anchor`, that reaches no section does not refuse the load: it is kept on the section that makes it as `:seon.doc/dead-links` (the target's address), every other section loads as usual, and while the target is still no stored section it is a derived `:dead-citation` problem naming the page, the section and the target ([health](health.md#a-dead-citation)); adding the target section closes it with nothing written (`seon.doc-test/a-link-to-no-section-is-a-dead-link-and-the-rest-loads`, `seon.root-test/a-dead-link-in-the-wiki-is-a-problem-and-the-boot-goes-on`). A schema key, a file citation and a name that names nothing are not stored as references, since there is no file entity; a name that names nothing stays in `:seon.doc/names`, and once it stops naming anything it is a `:dead-citation` ([health](health.md#a-dead-citation)). `seon.root/wiki-tx` takes a `baseline` for sections with no record of the code they were written against (```seon.root/wiki-baseline```, from git).

Cost, measured on a wiki of 8 pages, 60 KB and 60 sections on a test branch: a first load 12.6 ms and 17 MB, an unchanged reload 19 ms and 23 MB, of which the parse is 9 ms and 13 MB. Per section, an unchanged reload costs about 200 KB, most of it the seeks of what main holds (one per stated attribute, `seon.task/entity-tx`) (Datahike seeks on Jolt allocate ~33 KB each, `seon.db/avet-seek-allocates`); the proof is `seon.doc-cost-test/an-unchanged-reload-costs-a-parse-and-a-pull-per-section`. Measured 2026-10-29 on the wiki as it is (37 pages in the two dirs, 567 sections, 0.9 MB), an unchanged load is 217 MB, 92 M ticks and 0.25 s: the parse 54 MB, a seek or two for each of 4,284 citations and 567 sections the rest. It was 1.47 GB, 490 M ticks and 1.25 s, for three causes, each fixed at its source: each dir loaded twice (a link from one dir's page to another's was dead until the second pass; one load of both dirs resolves it at once, `seon.doc/load-dir-tx` over a vector of dirs); a section's names were `seon.ref/kept` over every citation, and a prose `path#anchor` is kept by parsing that file, so every such citation parsed a page (170 extra parses; `seon.doc/section-names` keeps only the names, the only citations it stores); and the reader per span (above). `seon.doc-cost-test/an-unchanged-reload-costs-a-parse-and-a-pull-per-section` has a section name a file's section by its address, which is 1.2 MB a section when a page is parsed for it. Root's boot loads nothing at all when main holds the digest of what the load reads: the pages, the program and the task ids (```seon.root/wiki-digest```, on root's fact as `:seon.root/wiki`; ```seon.root/wiki-boot-tx```). A boot after a change to the program or a page loads again (the load is a function of exactly that input), and ```my.root/load-docs!``` drops the digest, since it writes what the digest does not describe.

## Sections in a task's context

The page shows each wiki section the task, its needs or its agent cite as a part of its own, at the depth its cite asks for ([an entity cites](#an-entity-cites)): a link or one backtick shows the lead and the headings below, three backticks the section whole, a bare word its heading and lead's first sentence (```seon.render.ui/cite-model```, Markdown).

Beside them, the task's data's `:write-set` (```seon.context/task-data```, what ```my.task/data``` answers: its cites read by ```seon.task/task-cites```) shows each program var it cites with its source and its callers (```seon.doc/callers```, the program vars whose recorded definitions name it), and the wiki sections that cite it (```seon.doc/citing-sections```: their addresses, at most `:seon.config.ctx/cited-by`, the decisions page last: the sections the change owes an edit to and the ones that explain it), `:sections` each section it cites by ```seon.doc/view```, and `:to-prove` each proof with the form that runs it, or what the plan says it should assert when it is not written. Helpers the plan does not name are the agent's to ask for at its REPL (`my.agent/callees`, `my.agent/callers`, `my.agent/similar`): shown to every agent, they were mostly unrelated source. Stored sections keep no opening block, so a section whose first block is not a paragraph shows no lead at doc depth.

## A section read at a depth, with its edges

Every page and section is on main, reloaded when a page changes, and readable at three depths, each with the sections it cites and those citing it.
- ```seon.ref/shown``` is the one rule for a section at a depth; at :summary it is the heading and the lead's first sentence, and when the lead holds more, `:seon.ref/left` (its characters past that sentence) and `:seon.ref/read-at` (the address that reads it at :doc), so a summary says what it left (`seon.ref-test/a-summary-names-the-citation-that-reads-the-rest`).
- ```seon.doc/view``` shows a section at :summary, :doc or :code, and at every depth its :references (what its text cites, sections and vars) and :cited-by (the sections citing it), each at summary depth, from the stored edges (`seon.doc-test/a-view-at-summary-lists-references-and-cited-by`). ```seon.doc/page``` reads a page at a depth through the same view (`seon.doc-test/a-page-reads-at-a-depth`), and ```my.agent/help``` is that view: the help page at summary, any address at :code, a page's path at summary, and on a miss the nearest addresses (the help page's sections, pages named by a word of it and the sections the search finds for it, ```my.agent/help-nearest```) and the forms that read one, never nil (`seon.repl-test/help-reads-any-address-and-lists-on-a-miss`). A view costs the pulls of its section and its edges, flat in the wiki's size (`seon.doc-cost-test/a-view-costs-its-section-and-its-edges`).
- Every section has a lead, so its summary and its embedding say what it is about; one without is a `:leadless-section` ([health](health.md#a-section-with-no-lead), `seon.doc-test/every-section-of-the-wiki-has-a-lead`).

## Files and namespaces on demand

A namespace a cite names joins its entity (`:ns/name`): its part shows its doc and the form that lists its definitions, ```my.agent/doc``` of its name; only Seon's own `docs/` are stored as sections (above), and a file a text cites joins nothing.

A backticked dotted simple symbol (`seon.ref`) is a namespace candidate, resolve-only as v1's rule: ```seon.ref/kept``` keeps it when it names a namespace on the db (`:ns/name`) or one loaded, otherwise it stays prose (`seon.ref-test/a-namespace-is-read-as-a-namespace`). ```seon.ref/namespace-view``` shows it at the REPL by the one rule `seon.ref/shown`: the docstring is its lead and its definitions, privates included, are the sections below.
