# Cost proofs

**Cost is work done (ticks, bytes, calls), never wall or CPU time** (owner, 2026-10-23: "I like ticks vs wall clock or anything that is load dependent. Work done, not scheduled time slices").

A cost proof (`seon.cost/proof`) states what a function's work should cost as its input grows, measures it at a few sizes, and is red when a size costs past its estimate or grows steeper than the work needs, in a dimension that is counted exactly (```seon.cost/exact```: ticks, bytes and calls). A proof whose estimate states no exact dimension that was also measured is red (`NO COUNTED DIMENSION`), since time alone could never fail it. Sizes run ascending up to `:seon.config.proofs/cost-max-n` (at least the two smallest), and the run stops at the first size over its estimate. Time is measured, recorded and shown beside them, and is never red: it moves with the machine's load, while a green is reused forever at its reach digest ([merging](merging.md#a-merge-runs-what-the-change-reaches)), so a verdict must be a function of the code and its input alone (owner via root, 2026-10-23; before, a proof green on a quiet machine was reused under load and one judged under load went red: thread CPU 158 µs at load 10, 210 µs at load 17). Time's counted dimension is the engine's ticks (below).

## What is measured

A proof measures the function under test, on the thread that calls it, and nothing else in the process (task `seon.cost/a-proof-measures-only-its-own-thread`):
- **time** is that thread's CPU time, `seon.profile/cpu-ns` (CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID). Another thread's work and a pause the scheduler gave to someone else are not the call's. A call that waits (on a channel, a socket, a lock, a sleep) is measured by what it does, not by how long it waits: the waiting is its dependency's cost, proved on that dependency (`seon.profile-test/a-measure-is-the-same-beside-another-thread`: a call sleeping 20 ms reads under 2 ms). What the thread's time still holds: a collection that runs on it (the collector runs on the thread that triggers it), and how fast its core runs. Under load the scheduler moves it between the M1's performance and efficiency cores and the clock speed changes, so the same work costs more CPU time: 3.9 ms alone and 5.2 ms (1.34x) beside 16 busy threads, measured 2026-10-23, its wall time hardly more. So time over its estimate (3x) is a finding shown in the verdict (`TIME OVER (measured, not red)`), never a red; bytes and calls are exact;
- **bytes** are what that thread allocated, `seon.profile/alloc-bytes`: Chez's per-thread count on our branch ``jolt/thread-bytes`` (its allocation areas less the tails it dropped, and its large objects). Another thread's allocation, a collection's copying of live data, the conses the collector and the runtime's own books make on this thread (`during_alloc` raised: locked objects, guardians), what a collection or a collect request's handler allocates on the thread it lands on (Chez `$call-uncounted`, branch ``jolt/collect-uncounted``), and a new Scheme stack (on overflow, or a `call/1cc`, which a Jolt `try` is, finding the stack cache empty) are not counted. A collection empties every thread's stack cache, so a stack's bytes depend on what ran before, never on the call: counted, a `try` read 144 B warm and 65,664 B after a collection, and `seon.vector/near` read 65.5 KB more for each collection in its window, enough to turn its growth fit red under load (`seon.profile-test/a-collection-in-a-measured-call-is-not-its-bytes`, `seon.profile-test/a-new-stack-is-not-the-calls-bytes`). A stack's work is the call's depth, which its ticks count. Chez's own `(statistics)` and `bytes-allocated` count the whole process, so a proof under them reads whatever else ran beside it;
- **calls** are the contracted functions it called, counted exactly (`seon.profile`);
- **ticks** are the work the call did, the counted dimension for time (how they are counted: below). Load does not move them: over 30 runs in each of 6 processes, at load 21 and at 47 with four burners, every form counted the same ticks while thread CPU time over the same runs spread 36-860%; an extra inner loop that allocates nothing gave 2.56x the ticks with byte-identical allocation (worktree session pure-tests, 2026-10-23, `docs/source.md`). **The blind spot:** work done in C, a foreign call or a bulk copy, costs next to no ticks (a string concat measured 3000 ticks at 1 MB and at 4 MB while its CPU time grew 4x), and waiting costs none; bytes count the copies, which is why bytes stay beside ticks. ```seon.cost/once``` measures them (less ```seon.cost/tick-allowance``` per collection during the call), and the unit table prices an estimate's `:ops` in them.

**Ticks** are the other exact measure of a call's own work: `jolt.host/ticks-of` (Jolt `jolt/thread-ticks`) answers the engine-timer ticks a thunk ran, on its thread or its fiber, and `jolt.host/ticks` reads the running count. Jolt arms the timer in one place, `jolt-set-timer!`, which adds what was used of the last arm to the thread's count (a law: Jolt's `reference-code/jolt/test/chez/nested-budget-test.clj` is red unless that is the one `set-timer` in the host), and reads it whole (interrupts disabled by a plain pair: 0 bytes per read), without stopping or re-arming the timer. What it does not count: work run with interrupts disabled (Chez parks the timer there), and work while nothing is armed (`ticks-of` arms one for its extent). Quiet runs are exact: a 300,000-iteration loop read 300,007 ticks in 500 of 500 runs off a fiber, and 300,005 in a tick budget and on a fiber. The one error is a forced event, a collection or signal raised from another thread: its handler runs on this thread and its ticks count as this call's. Measured with 301 collections forced during each case (2026-10-23): +11 in 8 of 162,758 runs off a fiber, +26 or +27 in 10 of 168,143 in a budget, none in 46,900 on a fiber. A proof measuring ticks allows for that error: ```seon.cost/tick-allowance```, 64 ticks per collection during the call. What the count costs (measured at load 60, 2026-10-23): an empty `ticks-of` 3 ticks; a `run-interruptible` with no budget never reads the count (29 ticks, 225 ns, against 3 ticks, 4 ns for the plain call: its wind, handler and arm); a budget adds its reads, 4 ticks and about 40 ns.

Each size is the least of the calls at its steady state (```seon.cost/measure```: calls until `:seon.config.proofs/cost-settled` calls after the one that allocated the fewest bytes allocate no fewer (the first calls of a function allocate more; ticks and calls take their least over all of them), and a call that does not settle in `:seon.config.proofs/cost-max-samples` answers its least, marked `:unsettled` in the answer's meta; a count, never a time, so the minimum is a function of the code), less what measuring an empty call costs. The proofs: `seon.profile-test/a-threads-allocation-is-its-own`, `seon.profile-test/a-measure-is-the-same-beside-another-thread`.

## Counting calls

A contracted function is counted where it is called: ```seon.profile/counted``` wraps each armed function once more, counting every call exactly and timing one in 64, so a function's time is an estimate from its samples and its count is exact. Counts are kept per epoch: ```seon.profile/begin!``` opens one (an eval form, a cost measurement) and zeroes every count, ```seon.profile/calls``` is the epoch's total, ```seon.profile/touched``` the functions called since with their exact `:calls` and estimated `:ms` (it costs what was touched, never the program's size), and ```seon.profile/top``` the k costliest of them, by estimated ms then calls. An agent is one thread, so its counts are exact; root's procs run on several, where two threads can lose an increment, so root's counts are approximate and nothing reads them as proof.

**Every process arms the same functions.** Root arms `seon.contract/boot-nss` (seon.edn, seon.config, seon.contract, seon.profile, seon.print and its two parts) at boot, after their callers exist, and each test shard arms them before its tests (`test_runner/shard!`), so a proof counts the same contracted calls in `bin/test`, an agent's test child and the binary. Before 2026-10-30 only root did: `seon.doc/citing-sections` read 1 call under `bin/test` and 4 in an agent (its `seon.config/value`, `resolve` and `resolve-in`), and five proofs were green under `bin/test` and red in an agent's child. Arming them in the shard moved 22 estimates to the honest count (config reads: +1 to +3 each; printing a record through the armed printer: `seon.context/printed-run` 16 to 560, `seon.render/html` 19 to 144 a run). Proof: `seon.contract-test/a-test-process-arms-what-root-arms-at-boot`.

**Work set aside is no call's.** A signal handler runs at a safe point inside whatever call is running (seon.intr: root's SIGUSR1, a test child's SIGIO); its work is set aside (```seon.profile/aside```): its calls are not counted and its ticks and bytes count as a callee's to the call it interrupted, so an eval's profile holds the eval's calls only, never a handler's (a test child's SIGIO handler's `seon.sock/peer-gone?`).

## What an estimate allows and how a verdict reads

An estimate states its work's growth and its price per unit of that work. ```seon.cost/work``` is W(n), the growth's shape at n (constant, log, linear, n log n, quadratic, cubic); ```seon.cost/per-unit``` prices one unit of it, the estimate's `:ops` taken from the unit table and overridden by what `:per-n` states; ```seon.cost/expected``` is what the estimate allows at n in each dimension it states, fixed + per-unit x W(n). The dimensions are ```seon.cost/dimensions``` (ticks, time, bytes, calls, in the order a verdict shows them), and the exponent of a growth is ```seon.cost/exponent```, the least-squares slope of log cost over log size (1.0 linear, 2.0 quadratic), which the fit below compares with the work's own.

A proof runs the sizes ```seon.cost/sizes``` keeps (those up to the cap, ascending, the ones past it named on the verdict) and is refused when the cap leaves fewer than ```seon.cost/min-points```, three being the fewest that judge a growth on a curve and not on the line two points always make. ```seon.cost/proof``` asserts the estimate inside a deftest: one clojure.test report, pass or fail, whose message is the explanation below and whose `:actual` is the rows and verdict as data.

A verdict has one wording. ```seon.cost/verdict-lines``` writes it as [class words] lines, red first: within or over the estimate, each size over it, each dimension steeper than the work needs, loose and unmeasured dimensions as findings, an accepted overrun and a regression past it. ```seon.cost/explain``` joins the estimate, the measurements against it and those lines into the text an agent reads, and the page's verdict box shows the same lines.

## How growth is judged

Ticks allow one timer chunk. A measure of a call's ticks may read one 1,000-tick chunk more than another measure of the same call on the same input, plus about 50, whatever the load (`seon.cost-test/one-timer-chunk-is-not-a-red-and-a-real-regression-is`: 40 calls of one pinned transaction read 407,097 + 0..46 on 17 and + 1,041..1,048 on 23, no collection in any; the chunk's cause in Chez is open there). ```seon.cost/judge``` takes `:seon.config.proofs/tick-quantum` (1,050) off a size's ticks before judging it over its estimate, and against the growth before fitting it (the smallest size's up, the others' down), so one stray chunk is never a red while a real regression still is.

A dimension is steeper when its fitted growth exponent passes the estimate's growth by more than ```seon.cost/steeper-by``` over the same sizes. ```seon.cost/judge``` fits it so that a change making every size cheaper, or dearer, by one amount never moves the verdict (`seon.cost-test/a-speedup-never-reads-as-steeper`):
- with three sizes or more, on each size's increment over the smallest, m(n) - m(n0), against the work's own, W(n) - W(n0): no fixed part is assumed. Only when the growth is a real share of the cost (the largest increment at least ```seon.cost/growth-share``` of the smallest size's), and only the increments over ```seon.cost/increment-floor``` of the smallest size's. **Not for log work**: a log's cost in a stored index is a staircase (a level more every so many entries), and the increments of a staircase have no power-law shape: `diff-plan`'s bytes read 675.6k, 695.8k and 1.07M at 10, 100 and 1,000 forms (738k, 738k, 1.15M on another layout), n^1.29 and n^3.1 on increments against the n^0.60 a log allows, and n^0.19 on the cost less its fixed part. Log work takes the third basis below; a step cannot be told from a quadratic by three increments, only by the cost less its fixed part, and a pass over the data that stays under a log's estimate is red as over it at the next size (`seon.program-diff-test/a-diff-costs-the-change-never-the-program`, `seon.cost-test/growth-takes-the-basis-the-rows-allow`): a 0.5% variance in a measure moved a log row's increments, 71k on 406k ticks, by 0.02 of exponent, its whole margin;
- with two (a run capped at `:seon.config.proofs/cost-max-n` measures the two smallest), or when fewer than two increments are positive (a smallest size that cost more than the next), one increment has no shape, so the fixed part taken off is the estimate's `:fixed`, but never more than the smallest size leaves beyond its estimated per-unit part;
- for constant work, on the cost itself, which should not grow. This is looser than a fit above an accurate `:fixed`: a small per-size creep on a large base reads as flat, and only the largest size's own estimate judges it.

```seon.cost/growth``` answers each dimension's exponent, the one allowed and the basis it took. The shift leaves the verdict exactly as it was only in the counted dimensions (ticks, bytes, calls), where a size measures the same every run; an error in the smallest size's measure enters every increment.

Fitted on the stated `:fixed` taken off every size, a faster fixed part left the smallest size's remainder near zero, and the fit read a speedup as growth: reach-update on a faster Jolt, under its estimate at both sizes, read n^1.96.

## An accepted overrun and its ratchet

Cost is work done, counted (ticks, bytes, calls), never wall time (owner's ruling). A proof past its estimate stays green while the task its `:overrun` names is open and every dimension at the largest size is within `seon.cost/ratchet` (1.5x) of the `:measured` the task recorded, so a regression is red even inside an open overrun. A record is comparable only on the measure it was taken with: the proof's own thread's bytes and calls, exact and the same in every run, and that thread's CPU, which still moves with the machine's load. When the measure changes, every record is measured again on the new one, and its comment says when and under what load.

## How we measure

Every measurement, in a cost proof or outside one, starts from a written estimate and is read across sizes, never from one quick run (owner: "think about things algorithmically first and always put estimates on things"):
- **Estimate first.** Before the run, write what the work is proportional to (pages mapped, live bytes, datoms touched, forms compiled, bytes copied), the expected cost derived from that and the unit costs (`seon.cost/units`, never a previous run), its O(·), and what would show the model wrong.
- **Across sizes.** Measure at three or more sizes spanning 100x or more (a proof's default is 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000) and compare the fitted growth with the estimate's; a single size is never a result. Over repeats, report the least (a proof's choice), and the median and p95 when the spread matters; time anything under a microsecond in batches.
- **Measured over estimated.** Above a dimension's slack (`seon.cost/slack`: 3x for ticks and time, 1.25x for bytes, 1x for calls), or at most a third (`seon.cost/loose`), is a finding to explain (a wrong model, or the wrong work done) before the number is used: `seon.cost/judge`'s over and under; an over in an exact dimension is red, an under only a finding.
- **A process's memory is what the platform charges it**, `seon.lib/real-mb` over `jolt.host/process-footprint-bytes` (Jolt's: macOS's `phys_footprint` from `task_info`, one call to the kernel's ledger, what Activity Monitor and jetsam charge: private dirty pages, compressed ones included; on Linux the proportional set size of `/proc/self/smaps_rollup`, a shared page divided among the processes mapping it, so a forked child's inherited pages are shared between it and its parent). A forked child is charged every inherited anonymous page it touches, read or written, and the parent keeps its own charge (`seon.lib-test/the-memory-gauge-reads-what-macos-charges-across-a-fork`); only a write copies the page, so a read is charged but costs the machine nothing (measured 2026-10-29: four children each reading a parent's 200 MB were charged 201 MB each while the machine's free memory moved by noise, ~50 MB; writing it took ~1 GB). A sum of footprints over forked processes therefore overstates their RAM, so `bin/check scale` charges the machine instead: the drop in free plus inactive pages (`seon.lib/sysmem`, one `host_statistics64` call in `native/probe.c` in `native/probe.c`) from before its agents start to their peak, per agent, times `:seon.config.agent/max-running`, plus root's and the fork server's own footprints, against `:seon.config.machine/memory-mb` (task seon.lib/scale-charges-what-the-machine-lost). Measured 2026-10-29 with 100 agents at load 10: their footprints summed to 9,995 MB (100 MB each, `:agent-real-mb-sum`) while the machine's available memory fell 20.6 MB per agent, so root 649 MB + fork server 133 MB + ten agents is 988 MB by the machine's drop and 1,782 MB by footprints (`:footprint-need-mb` reports the footprint figure beside the checked one). The machine-wide reading is moved by every process on it (other apps, the compressor, reclaimed cache), so it is read as a drop over a whole run of many agents and judged against a bound in GB; a test of it is recorded, never judged to the MB: `seon.lib-test/the-memory-gauge-reads-what-macos-charges-across-a-fork` asserts the ledger's charge (a reading child and a writing child of a parent's 200 MB are each charged 204 MB) and prints the machine's drops, which at load 10 were 15, 1 and -4 MB for the reader and 0, 133 and 156 MB for the writer (its 200 MB, with inactive pages reclaimed meanwhile). A sum of a process's private resident pages is not a gauge: a fork turns the parent's pages copy-on-write, and they leave the sum. `bin/check scale` and an agent's view read `jolt.host/process-footprint-bytes` ([machine](machine.md#the-target-a-16-gb-macbook-m1)).
- **Record the load** beside a timed run, and take final numbers on a quiet machine: time moves with the machine's load (above), bytes and calls do not.

**A proof's input is the shape production holds.** Sample data that differs from production in what the measured code depends on makes the numbers lie (AGENTS §5). The test store is in memory: its values keep no index node at an address, so `datahike.api/datoms-diff` cannot skip the subtrees two values share and walks every datom of the range it is given. A function whose cost rests on that skip is measured on a store of its own on disk, the one-store rule's last resort, with that reason where it is made (```seon.reconcile-test/observed-input```, ```seon.program-cost-test/on-disk-diverged-of```, ```seon.merge-cost-test/three-of```). On the test store the reconciler's index observe read 4.71M ticks and 17.9 MB at 10 tasks; on LMDB it reads 1.11M and 5.8 MB, flat to 1,000.

A cost proof that names its function only as a quoted symbol keeps a stale stored result, since a quoted name is data and no reference ([source](source.md#tests-run-by-reach)): its digest moves only when the test's own text does.

**A measure is taken in the multi-threaded regime.** Jolt's lazy seq cells take a claim path (compare-and-swap) from the first real OS thread on, for the rest of the process (`jolt-mt?`, never reset): a seq over a String's chars reads 8 ticks and 80 B a character before it and, since Jolt 546c3d1f (a pure lazy producer such as ``str-seq`` is claim-free when threaded, and the claim path drops a box), 11 ticks and 80 B after (15 and 272 before that commit); ``clojure.string/escape`` 44.7 and 292 B before, 60.6 and 436 B after (58.7 and 676 before that commit). Every Seon root is threaded, so the second is the cost production pays; ``seon.cost/measure`` starts one thread before its first call so a proof's numbers do not depend on whether an earlier test in the process made one (a `future`, a shell call: ``seon.config-test/a-checkouts-local-config-is-a-layer-only-when-named``). Found 2026-10-29: ``seon.cost-test/the-unit-table-is-current`` alone read the first pair and in a shard the second, and the table holds the second.

**A proof leaves the process as it found it.** Contracted calls are counted on armed vars, so a test that arms a namespace (`contract/instrument!`) and leaves it armed adds its checks to every proof after it in the shard: `seon.print-cost-test/armed-printing-adds-only-its-contracted-calls` left `seon.print` armed, and `seon.render-cost-test/a-new-turn-costs-one-turns-messages` counted 609 calls in a shard against 420 alone (bisected by pairs: `bin/test --all --set :seon.config.proofs/jobs=1 A B`). The test now reloads what it armed when it found it unarmed.

## A bound in the contract

A function's contract may carry its cost bound beside its schema: `:seon.cost/bound`, a plain function of the same arguments answering what one call may cost, `{:ticks :bytes :calls}` (any of them) and `:out`, the measure of its answer that a caller's bound passes on to the next callee (```seon.cost/bound``` reads it). The bound is the algorithm sketched: its terms are the steps the function takes on these arguments, each at its own cost, measured once on a case that isolates that step. Priced from the unit table instead, ```seon.ctx/changes```' bound was 1.6 to 7x under its ticks and up to 1,000x under its bytes. A key it visits costs 927 ticks and 2,980 B (sorted gets through the members' comparator, the members? scan, distinct over concat), where its unit operations price about 60 ticks; a change costs 3,788 ticks and 30.8 KB, most of it its path printed for the sort.

A generated estimate (`:seon.cost/generated`: `:of`, `:n`, `:work`, `:cases`, and `:gen`, the var of a test.check generator of argument vectors) is a cost proof with no sizes: ```seon.cost/cases``` draws `:cases` argument vectors from `:gen`, or from the contract's input by malli's generator, at the running test's seed (```seon.proofs/test-seed```, so the same code draws the same cases); ```seon.cost/run``` measures the function on each (```seon.cost/measure```) against its bound on the same arguments, and ```seon.cost/judge``` judges each case as it judges a size (over, at the same slack, red on a counted dimension; loose on the case with the greatest bound). Growth is not fitted: the bound states it. A case over its bound is shown with the arguments that reproduce it. Proofs: `seon.cost-test/a-bound-holds-on-its-contracts-generated-inputs`, `seon.cost-test/a-scan-inside-the-loop-is-red-under-the-bound`, `seon.ctx-test/changes-holds-its-bound-on-generated-records` (200 generated records, each after up to five edits that share what they leave as the patch does; a copy of changes that skips nothing goes over it).

A bound composes: a caller's bound is the sum of its callees' bounds over the measures they pass on in `:out`. The render pipeline's later-turn request (until 2026-10-29 a diff of two pages: `build`, `advance` and a page draw twice; now the live walk's parts), with each piece's bound linear in the session's measures (evals, cited vars, runs), predicted the measured ticks and bytes at 0.83 to 1.08x over 25 generated session shapes. Two things a bound must state: a cache is an input (a page draw costs a quarter as much warm as cold on one shape, so a bound over a memoized function takes the cache's state, or bounds the cold call only), and a bound is written in the measures its generator varies, so what the generator leaves out is invisible to it (a real agent's measured request was 6x the shape-only bound, since it holds search vectors, proof sources and large eval values the sessions lacked). The sound bound for `changes` was about 3x loose at its median, because its per-key constant was calibrated on a 1,000-member collection.

A function that takes a db has no generated input yet: `:seon.host/db` is a predicate, so its cases need a domain generator (a session's shape over a seeded db), and its bound is written in that generator's measures (task `seon.cost/a-db-function-draws-its-cases-from-a-domain-generator`).

## What a contract costs

A contract is checked on every call, so the schema of a value that many calls take costs that value's size on each of them. A large value passed among helpers is checked shallowly (its keys and their types, never each entry), and each entry is checked once, where it enters: `:seon.reconcile/index` (every reconciler helper takes it; put-task checks a task as `:seon.task/own`, put-run a run as `:seon.run/run`) and `:seon.ctx/ix` (the ctx walk's state). Checked deeply, the index's listing cost tasks x tasks; its proof is `seon.reconcile-test/an-index-is-listed-at-the-cost-of-its-tasks`.

## What a test run measures

Every test run measures what each contracted function cost, from the same counters every call already passes through: ```seon.profile/counted```, the Chez wrapper every armed function has, counts each call and the engine ticks and thread bytes it did, in all and of its own (its callees' taken off by two process-wide counters each call saves, zeroes and adds back), with no clock read and nothing allocated a call. Each test opens its own epoch (```seon.proofs/run-tests!```, through ```seon.profile/measure!```), so at its end ```seon.profile/touched``` is that test's functions, which go on its result as `:fns` and into its cached verdict, once a digest ([merging](merging.md#verdicts-are-a-cache)); nothing is written a call, nor a (test, function) pair. Each test's ended line also carries `:heap`, the live heap its child holds after a collection as the test ends (```seon.agent/take-line``` keeps it per test, and the run's answer holds `:heap {test bytes}`): what one test keeps for the tests after it in the same child, which the agent's heap ceiling (`:seon.config.agent/heap-extra-bytes`) bounds. It is recorded, never judged.

Measured 2026-10-27 (load 30-50): a call of a two-key predicate 10 ticks; counting calls only (the wrapper before) 11; calls, ticks and bytes 15, about 60 ns more in wall time; the same predicate contracted, its schema checked, 48 ticks and 120 ns. Over the 64 tests of `seon.proofs-test`, `seon.ctx-test` and `seon.merge-test`: 508,005 contracted calls in 6.55G ticks, so the 4 ticks more a call are 0.03% of the tests' work (about 30 ms of wall in all), and 2,605 (test, function) entries, about 40 a test, each about 55 bytes printed in the run's detail. Proofs: `seon.profile-test/a-functions-own-work-is-its-work-less-its-callees`, `seon.profile-test/counting-a-call-allocates-nothing-and-costs-a-few-ticks`. Self is approximate where a call throws (the frame that catches counts the thrower's callees as its own) and, in bytes, where a call parks on a fiber.

These measures are the data for a function's cost bound (```seon.cost/bound```): what one call of it costs on the inputs the tests give it.

## A diff per attribute costs a descent each

`d/datoms-diff` of one attribute's range skips the subtrees two commits share, but each call still descends from both roots and opens the unshared leaf, a fixed ~160 KB measured on the test store (2026-10-29), whatever the attribute holds. `seon.reconcile/index` made one call for each of the 90 attributes the watched namespaces declare: 14.6 MB at 25 tasks and 20.1 MB at 50 for a move of one datom (`seon.render-cost-test/a-move-of-main-costs-what-moved`, estimate 16 MB, red once the schema grew past it: the cost followed the schema, which that test's digest did not, [source](source.md#tests-run-by-reach)). A caller that read the diff already (`seon.render.proc/advance-main` reads main's one `:eavt` diff) hands it over, and the index takes its watched datoms: 9 KB, 3 calls at 25, 50 and 100 tasks. A caller with no diff in hand (the turn, the fork server) still pays the descents, so reads the diff once and passes it when it can.

**The branch's views read one diff and each reader takes what it needs from it** (2026-10-29, task branch-diff-cost). Measured on a branch of two vars and 18 evals on Seon's own program (read from the other session's root; baseline reading the branch db 0.16M ticks): `seon.merge/content-datoms` base to branch 3.4M ticks and 10.6 MB for the 45 content attributes (an empty attribute costs ~15K ticks and 88 KB, one with datoms 100-350K and 300-970 KB; `:db/txInstant`, one datom per transaction and no key's content, was the costliest at 348K, and its 87 transaction entities were read again by `touched-by`: it is no content attribute now), and `seon.problems/caused-split` 6.9M ticks and 40 MB, which its docstring called flat in what the branch did not touch and was not: it read every section's seven attributes, every var name and task id, every catching form and every task plan on both sides (the scans were 0.6M, 0.5M, 1.2M ticks each). The whole `:eavt` diff of the same branch is no cheaper (6.0M ticks, 13.9 MB: every unshared leaf's datoms are candidates, each decided by a seek in both whole sets), so the descents are the floor of a caller that has no diff, and the saving is in reading it once. `content-datoms` and `caused-split` (and `caused`) take the datoms that differ (a caller that read the `:eavt` diff passes it: `content-datoms` filters it to the content attributes in one pass, `caused-split` looks each section, name, form and task it touches up by entity on both sides); without them they read their own, `caused-split`'s the 12 attributes it reads (`seon.problems/caused-datoms`). Given the diff, `caused-split` costs the old-word table and the changed places (about 0.45M ticks and 1.8 MB at 10, 100 and 1,000 untouched sections and catch sites, `seon.problems-test/caused-follows-what-changed`, in-memory test dbs; 0.69M and 2.8 MB at 10 before, linear in them), and `content-datoms` 3.7K ticks, 5.3 KB and 3 calls (`seon.program-cost-test/content-datoms-given-a-diff-are-a-filter`, on LMDB). A name that appeared or vanished finds the sections citing it by `:seon.doc/names`, now an AVET index, not by scanning every citation (a scan was 0.46M ticks at 1,657 citations). Not measured: the live root's `caused-split` after the change (it runs the old code); the agent page's standing diff, which will pass the one diff, is another task.

## Where it is used

An agent's task names its cost proofs as goals ([agent](agent.md)); `bin/test` runs them with every other test; a proof's `:ops` are priced in ticks, bytes and time from the unit table `seon.cost/units`, which `seon.cost-test/the-unit-table-is-current` measures again and holds to 1.25x. Clocks that order events are `seon.profile/now-ns` ([machine](machine.md#the-clock)); `cpu-ns` orders nothing ([clocks](clocks.md)).

**An evaluation records its cost once, when it ran, and shows it from that fact.** `seon.repl/wrap-cost` puts the form's `:ticks`, `:ms`, `:alloc-bytes` and costliest functions (by ticks, each with its calls, ticks and bytes, in all and its own) beside the answer, read from the one measurement of a call, ```seon.profile/measure!``` (which a cost proof's `seon.cost/measure` reads too: a call that throws is measured and its exception goes on; `seon.main-test/only-measure-reads-the-tick-counter` holds it the only reader of the tick counter in src); the reply carries them and `record-tx` stores the same values. Nothing measures it again, so what the model reads of an eval's cost is the same bytes in every later turn and the provider's prompt cache holds (DECISIONS, 2026-10-23). Proof `seon.repl-test/an-evals-response-carries-its-ticks`. Printing it in the context and the page is the render's: its proof, that the rendered cost is the same bytes in every later turn, belongs to the render that prints `:ticks` (agent-view).
