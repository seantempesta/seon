## Drive 3 — SIGKILL during parallel work

The crash wave deliberately occupied both recovery positions:

- three `F4_SLOW_CALL` requests reached the recording proxy and completed at
  the upstream model, but their responses were held for 30 seconds; and
- three `F4_FAST_EVAL` requests committed real model attempts and one
  `(loop [] (recur))` form each, leaving three live eval receipts.

The parent observed six recorded requests, six prompt captures, three
attempt facts, three running receipts, and all six fleet rows mid-turn before
issuing `kill -9` to the JVM. Recovery produced:

- trigger to recorded provider arrival: 425–443 ms;
- trigger to running eval receipt: 2,306–2,392 ms; and
- kill-ready marker to recovered-ready marker: approximately 10 seconds,
  including a fresh JVM start and store reopen.

- six recovered runs and six recovery operations;
- exactly three receipts with `:seon.cluster.eval/interrupted-at`;
- three attempts and six captures, unchanged from the pre-crash census;
- six requests in the external recorder before kill and still six after
  recovery — zero duplicate paid calls; and
- no second form or receipt for any old run.

Only after that census did the parent commit six explicit outside re-wakes.
All six agents returned their own `<agent>-adapt-ok` result in
2,622–2,697 ms. The recorder then contained exactly twelve requests:
six original plus six explicit re-wakes, with zero authorization headers.

This distinguishes the two honest crash cases. A run killed inside SCI has a
running receipt that recovery interrupts. A run killed before the provider
result is committed has a prompt capture but no attempt or form; recovery
settles it without inventing either and without repeating the external call.
