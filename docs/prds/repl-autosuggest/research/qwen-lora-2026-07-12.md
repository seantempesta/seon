# Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B LoRA — frontier-draft finetune vs its own zero-shot bar

**STATUS: HALTED (owner stop order, 2026-07-12 evening) — training
complete, eval deferred to display v3.** The display in both the training
data and the v1/v2 eval rows is defective (malli specs stripped from
cards, glyph tax, stale cards); a v3 display re-export is coming and any
conclusion drawn on v2 would be discarded. Everything below the
data-generation section that reports scores is labeled **v2-display
numbers — observations, not conclusions**. The adapter, the data
pipeline, and the drafts file are the durable deliverables; re-run the
eval matrix after the v3 re-export (and regenerate training pairs from
the same v3 display before any adapter comparison is trusted).

**Date:** 2026-07-12 · **Question:** does a LoRA finetune on ~600
curated frontier-draft pairs beat the stock 1.5B's own measured bars —
instr-zero .152 and instr-few .265/.383
([[kt3b-coder-models-2026-07-12.md]]) — and what does it do to the three
named weaknesses (parse .877, 4× spurious ids, register .100)? ·
**Model:** `mlx-community/Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct-4bit` (QLoRA) ·
**Eval rows:** the held-out acme sets (v1 `data/tune/acme-2026-07-12.jsonl`,
v2 `acme-2026-07-12-v2.jsonl`) — NEVER trained on · **Scorers:** legacy
`kt3_score.clj` (KT3b parity) + the extended KT3-redux mode (set-union
best-match F1 over `target_bundle`, error decomposition) · **Spend:**
$0.24 DeepSeek (draft generation); training + eval local MLX, $0.

## TL;DR

- **Data pipeline SHIPPED and leakage-proven**: 685 staged situations
  (15 fresh domains, arc/kt2b/abstain families) → 595 DeepSeek drafts
  ($0.24) → mechanical curation (parse/fn-exists/id-ingredients/idiom
  gates + narrow repairs) → **647 training pairs** (251 draft-verbatim,
  52 repaired, 254 mechanical-fallback, 90 abstention). 0 shared ids and
  0 shared 8-grams with the held-out rows, verified both directions.
- **Training worked mechanically**: QLoRA rank 16, 500 iters, val loss
  **1.127 → 0.065**; adapter at
  `src-needle/checkpoints/lora-qwen25c15b-instr/`.
- **Partial eval (ONE completed arm, v2-display numbers)**: zero-shot
  LoRA on the 4-card framing = useful **.198** vs stock zero-shot .152
  (+30%), substantive **.233 vs .152**, next-form head-match **.271 vs
  .177**, plan-kind credit **.311 vs .094**, register **.176 vs .029**,
  and the row-9 register bundle now emits per-attribute registrations
  (the truncation weakness) — but **parse collapsed .895 → .593** (39
  repetition-loops to cap + 48 malformed literals), which caps overall
  useful. Among rows that PARSE, useful = **.334** — above the .265
  stock few-shot bar. Reliability, not substance, is the binding
  failure; exactly the gate the design's constrained decoding owns.
- **The coverage gradient VANISHED under the LoRA** (Pearson −.04; flat
  ~.20 across tranches vs +.29 stock): the adapter learned situational
  priors that substitute for missing ingredients (lo-coverage tranche
  .02 → .21, a 10× lift) while syntax brittleness gives back the
  hi-coverage tranche (.24 → .19). Situation-priors are also where the
  fabrication risk lives — see the `:my.expisode/` namespace fusion.
- **One mechanical driver bug found and fixed for everyone**: the
  mlx-community 4-bit conversions ship `eos_token_id: 151643`
  (`<|endoftext|>`) only, no generation_config.json; a chat-trained
  adapter that ends its turn at `<|im_end|>` (id 151645) therefore NEVER
  stops and burns the 2048 cap (~60 s/row). Fix: register `<|im_end|>`
  as an eos at load (upstream Qwen lists both) — landed in
  `kt3b_coder_models.py` and `kt3_redux.py`; stock arms are
  score-unaffected (they emit `<|endoftext|>` right after `<|im_end|>`).

## Data generation — frontier-draft mode (design.md §Data sources)

Pipeline (all under `src-needle/scripts/`, run in order):

1. **`lora_gen_situations.py`** — 685 staged situations whose SHAPE
   mirrors the held-out eval contexts' render grammar (plan block +
   transcript block, event/result/clip-glyph grammar reproduced from the
   projection) but whose CONTENT is fresh. Three families:
   - **arc** (450): 15 domains × 3 variants × 10 stages — fresh
     plan-lay-down → schema `register!` bundle → seed `transact!` →
     report query → plan bookkeeping (done/active, discovered `step!`) →
     inspection → stuck-error recovery → planner-consult → finish.
     Domains: recipes, garden plantings, invoices, mileage, podcasts,
     houseplants, game nights, bird sightings, tool loans, donations,
     subscriptions, tea brews, chores, films, errands — none of the
     held-out scenario nouns (expenses, books/reading log, team facts,
     standup tasks).
   - **kt2b** (145): single-ask turns re-minted from the KT2b case
     machinery (`cases/kt2b_cases.json` — BFCL v4 live_simple/
     live_irrelevance, tau2 user-turn style, agentbench/terminal-bench
     phrasings re-domained onto the fn index), fresh ids, the case's
     expected fn kept as a mechanical intent stamp. Covers the wide fn
     surface (fs, shell, web, blob, kb, canvas, messaging, lifecycle).
   - **abstain** (90, ~13%): nothing-applies situations (answered user +
     empty frontier + outbound summary sent; or `complete` already
     returned `:idle`) → target is EMPTY — the model learns to emit
     nothing (an immediate `<|im_end|>`; verified 2 completion tokens
     through the chat template).
   Context sizes: p50 465 / p90 649 / max 707 est-tokens (matches the
   held-out ≤678 envelope).

2. **Leakage guard** (mechanical, hard-fails generation; re-verified
   over the final training files):
   - id disjointness: 873 fresh `XXX-26########` ids ∩ 541 held-out ids
     (v1+v2, contexts+targets+meta) = **0**, both directions.
   - content 8-grams: **0** generated user-message bodies share any
     8-word-gram with any held-out user-message body. Two of the first
     phrasings tripped this ("derived from the stored rows, not
     recomputed by hand"; the planner-consult preamble) and were
     reworded — the guard caught both. Render-grammar scaffold (plan
     coaching lines, transcript headers, event brackets) is exempt: it
     is the projection's constant grammar, identical at serving time.
   - the held-out scenario nouns are banned as generated domain nouns.

3. **`lora_draft_deepseek.py`** — DeepSeek-v4-pro drafts the next
   form(s) per non-abstain situation under the EXACT KT3 prompt shape
   (context + cards bracket + the terse instruction), thinking disabled
   (the KT3 gotcha), temp 0. **595 drafts, 483,180 prompt + 35,515
   completion tokens = $0.24.**

4. **`lora_curate.py`** (+ `lora_forms.clj`, the bb/edamame per-form
   analyzer) — mechanical gates; drafts are raw material, never gold:

   | gate | action | hits |
   |---|---|---|
   | parse (edamame) | reject | 6 |
   | def/defn in draft | reject (v0 kind exclusion) | 4 |
   | square-peg heads (swap!/reset!/atom/throw/try/println/…) | reject | 0 |
   | hallucinated fn (head ∉ index ∪ ns-move ∪ clojure.core; nested incl.) | reject | 14 |
   | spurious id (id-string ∉ context — the ingredients rule) | reject | 6 |
   | bare/unknown my.plan arg keys (vocab computed from `src/my/plan.cljs`) | reject | 1 |
   | `register!` unqualified attr / entity-`[:map]` style | reject → per-attr idiom | 72 |
   | wrong intent (no intended head emitted) | reject | 187 |
   | `transact!` bare vector arg | REPAIR: wrap `{:seon.db/tx-data …}` | 26 |
   | bare `:tx-data` key | REPAIR: `:seon.db/tx-data` | 3 |
   | keyword-valued ids (`:seon.agent/id :my.agent.X…`) | REPAIR: string | ~4 |
   | in-ns/require duplicating the gold prefix | REPAIR: dedupe | counted per-row |

   Rejections at arc stages with a **mechanical fallback target**
   (correct-by-construction: reconcile!-markdown compiled from the
   STRUCTURED numbered user message — the narrowly sanctioned use; the
   per-attribute register! bundle; the seed transact!; done!/active!;
   step!; list-open; document; message/user + complete) keep the
   fallback instead of dying; kt2b and report stages have no fallback —
   a non-matching draft is rejected outright. Notable draft failure
   modes (kept in the file as negative examples): `(transact! [maps…])`
   without the request-map shape; entity-map `register!` with
   `:recipe/name`-style wrong-namespace keys; `done!`/`active!`
   re-issued where the situation calls for the report query or the
   finish summary.

   **Yield: 685 situations → 251 kept-verbatim + 52 kept-repaired + 254
   kept-mechanical + 90 abstain = 647 pairs; 38 rejected.**

   Committed drafts file (all 685 rows, kept AND rejected, per-row
   status/repairs/draft_raw provenance, v2 row shape):
   **`data/tune/drafts-deepseek-2026-07-12.jsonl`**.

5. **Training splits** (gitignored, derived from the drafts file):
   `src-needle/data/lora/{train,valid}.jsonl` — 619/28 rows, mlx chat
   format, user = the byte-exact KT3 drafting prompt, assistant = the
   curated target. True chat-token lengths p50 936 / p90 1202 / max
   1487 (0 rows over the 2560 cap). A parallel completions-format pair
   (`cont/`) stages the base-continuation arm (cont-document prompt →
   FIRST form) — **not trained** (halted before that arm).

### The three named weaknesses (v2-display deltas)

- **Parse rate — REGRESSED**: .593 vs the .877/.895 stock band. Failure
  split: 39/214 temp-0 repetition loops to the 2048 cap
  (done!/active! alternating; truncated mid-string ⇒ unparseable) + 48
  clean-stop rows with malformed literals (odd-count maps —
  `:my.expisode/not-soon` missing its value; double-slash keywords
  `:my.kb/book/id`; `)`-for-`]` bracket errors). The adapter's Clojure
  is less well-formed than stock's OOD — small-data LoRA buys substance
  and costs syntax reliability. This is precisely the layer the
  design's constrained-decoding trie owns (symbol/keyword/delimiter
  clamping); unconstrained greedy is the wrong serving mode for it.
- **Spurious ids — better than few-shot, worse than zero-shot**: value
  recall **.324** (best of any arm measured: DeepSeek .250, stock few
  .240, stock zero .216) with **39 spurious** ids (uniform lens) vs
  stock zero 17, DeepSeek 13, stock few 51. The redux decomposition
  splits the LoRA's spurious ids: **58 grounded-in-context vs 7
  invented** (bundle lens) — the ids are real ids from the row's
  context placed on the wrong call, NOT exemplar leakage (the KT3b
  few-shot failure mode) and rarely fabricated. The ingredient-gated
  training data (every id in every pair appears in its context) shows
  up here.
- **Register kind — the biggest win**: mean credit **.176** vs stock
  zero .029 / stock few .059 (and above DeepSeek's all-rows .162). Row
  9's target (five per-attribute `register!` forms) now gets a full
  per-attribute bundle (.73 useful; stock few-shot emitted a lone
  `in-ns` for .25). The per-attribute idiom enforced by curation
  transferred. Caveat inside the win: the attr NAMESPACE is fused —
  `:my.expisode/…` (expense × episode, a training-domain token) — the
  overfit signature of 15 training domains; more domain diversity (or
  keyword-position constrained decoding over context tokens) is the
  fix.

### Coverage tranches — the gradient vanished

| arm | <.25 (40) | .25–.75 (64) | ≥.75 (106) | Pearson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stock instr-zero | .020 | .086 | .242 | +.30 |
| stock instr-few | .075 | .187 | .398 | +.37 |
| DeepSeek | .105 | .183 | .365 | +.29 |
| **LoRA instr-zero** | **.207** | **.220** | **.190** | **−.04** |

Every previous arm reproduced KT3's coverage law (score rises with
ingredient coverage). The LoRA is FLAT: it lifts the low-coverage
tranche 10× (situational priors — it knows what KIND of form a
bookkeeping/schema/finish turn wants even without ingredients) and
loses the high-coverage tranche to the parse failures. Two readings,
both actionable post-v3: (a) priors help exactly where the projection
is weakest — complementary to ingredient fixes; (b) priors emitted
without ingredients are where wrong-id/wrong-value risk concentrates —
the serving gate (validate ids against the live db) is load-bearing.

### Latency (v2-display, under heavy GPU contention)

Wall p50 **2.07 s** / mean 12.8 s — the mean is dominated by the 39
repetition-loop rows burning the 2048-token cap (p90 56 s). Median
generation 55 tokens at ~38 tok/s decode, prompt ~1.1k tokens, peak
2.27 GB. With the eos fix + a 512-token serving cap (the design's
decoder budget), a healthy suggestion is ~1–3 s in this contention
regime; KT3b measured 0.28 s p50 for the same model uncontended.

### Not measured (halted)

3-shot-LoRA (do exemplars still help post-LoRA?), the full-index
context arms (LoRA vs stock under the owner's fair-test framing — stock
baselines exist from the parallel redux lane), the base-continuation
LoRA arm (completions splits staged), and checkpoint selection (only
iter-500 evaluated).

## Verdict — provisional, v2-display

**Does LoRA-on-~600-drafts beat exemplars?** Not yet as served:
zero-shot LoRA (.198) beats zero-shot stock (.152) everywhere except
parse rate, and beats the .265 few-shot bar ON THE ROWS IT PARSES
(.334), but the 40% parse failure floor keeps the headline below the
bar. The failure is concentrated and mechanical (repetition loops +
malformed literals) — the two known fixes are the design's constrained
decoding (§B1: "even on its home task, unconstrained greedy garbles")
and more/cleaner data. **What would 5k pairs plausibly buy?** The three
observed failure modes scale differently: domain-token fusion
(`:my.expisode/`) and the flat query kind are data-diversity problems
that 5k pairs with more domains/query variety should largely fix;
malformed literals should improve with scale but constrained decoding
fixes them outright for ~0 extra cost; the repetition loops are a
stopping-distribution problem that more abstention/varied-length data
helps but a 512-token serving cap already contains. Given the $0.24
data cost measured here, a 5k-pair set costs ~$2 of drafting — cheap
enough to run after the v3 display lands rather than speculate.

**Standing findings independent of the display defect**: the data
pipeline + leakage guard + curation gates work end-to-end; the eos
driver fix; the register-idiom transfer; grounded-not-invented id
errors under ingredient-gated training; the coverage-gradient flattening
as the signature of situational-prior learning.

- Training contexts are TEMPLATE-generated in the render grammar, not

- 254/647 pairs are mechanical-fallback targets (correct by

- The stop-order rescores use the extended kt3_score.clj mode landed by
  the parallel redux lane (uncommitted at time of writing) — rerun
  `lora_rescore_4card.py` if that scorer moves.
