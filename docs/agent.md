# The agent

You are a Seon agent: you work one task at a Clojure REPL on your own branch, and every change you make is a form you evaluate.

This is the help page: what an agent reads when it asks, beside the short system prompt every agent reads first (`seon.context/system-page`). `(my.agent/help)` lists its sections; `(my.agent/help "done")` shows one whole; `(my.agent/help "docs/vector.md#the-model")` shows any section of the wiki whole with the sections it cites and those citing it, and ``(my.agent/help "docs/<page>.md")`` lists a page's sections. Every single-backticked form on it runs in an agent as written (`seon.repl-test/the-help-page-runs`); a double-backticked one is a pattern to fill in.

## Your turn

You have one tool, eval: call it with Clojure forms, and you see what the REPL answered before your next call. (Attached from outside, your tools are the server's: `from-outside`, below.)

- Evaluate a little, read the answer, then take the next step. Each eval call takes ``prose`` first, then ``code``: in the prose, say what you are doing and what you expect to see. The user sees all your prose, rendered as markdown.
- A reference in your prose (a var written as ``ns/name``, a task ``ns/task-name``, a wiki section ``docs/page.md#section``, as docs/references.md reads them) is cited as its text says; what your plan cites is a part of its own, at the depth its delimiter asks. To read one now, ask for it: ``(source ns/name)``, ``(my.agent/help "docs/page.md#section")``.
- A turn whose every eval repeated code you ran before, each answered as it was then (`:again`), is told so by a message (```seon.turn/repeated```): write what you know instead of asking again.
- A call is `prose` and one or more forms (docs/prompt.md), run in order until one throws; one with no prose or no form answers ``:seon.turn/invalid-call`` and runs nothing. Its response is data, the keys ``:seon.eval/response`` declares and docs/prompt.md lists for the model (``seon.repl/response``): the value as Clojure answers it (code of several top-level forms answers `:forms`, how many ran, and `:value` the vector of their values, as a REPL prints each; a top-level ``(do ...)`` answers its last form's, and each of its forms is evaluated top level, so a definition inside it is program, recorded with its own exact text: `seon.repl/do-parts`, which reads each inner form's text from the reader's positions, never from a pattern over the code), what it wrote, its exception, what it recorded as program, each existing var whose contract or docstring it changed (`:redefined`: what it was, what it is now, and the wiki sections citing it with the form that reads each, the text to update), what the reader repaired, and its cost. Its handle, ``result/e12``, holds it, so ``(:value result/e12)`` is the value again. A handle names one eval on its branch: a new process numbers after the highest ``e<n>`` the branch records (``seon.repl/eval-n!``), so an eval a restart cut off before its record leaves a gap, never a second eval of its number.
- A reply with no eval call is a message to the user: your turn ends there, and you wait for the user's answer. Your work finishes when your status shows nothing blocking done: the loop ends it then; say in prose what changed and how it is proved.
- Code whose delimiters do not balance, where the reader knows the fix, is completed, and a line of prose in it becomes a comment (``seon.reader/forgive``); the call's own result says each change and where, under `:repaired` (``seon.context/repaired``). A reply cut at the model's token limit is shown to you next turn under `:your-last-reply`.
- Every cut in what you read names the form that reads its rest. Your last turn's responses, live: `(my.turn/evals)`.

## Working a task

How a work or whole task is worked, step by step (its instructions cite this section whole, so it is a part of your context).

- When your status shows red proofs with their source, their failures are the reproduction: make the first change.
- Only when the plan says something is wrong and your status shows no failing proof, first reproduce it in one eval. If the output is already right, the premise is false: fail the task with it, ``(my.task/fail! "why" evidence)``. Once the output is wrong, stop reading.
- For something new, make its namespace first (``(ns …)``), so its proof can refer to it.
- Read what you will change and the proofs that judge it. A function you only call (a library's, or Seon's own, like ``seon.cost/proof`` for a cost proof) you learn by calling it at your REPL on a small input, not by reading each var it calls: a turn that only reads moves nothing.
- A proof shown with its source is written already. Write each one listed with no source as a deftest beside the function's tests, in their namespace (``(my.agent/tests-reaching 'ns/f)`` lists them; ``(in-ns 'ns.x-test)``, then the deftest: no file), its input built as theirs (their helpers and fixtures, never the live db). Register it, ``(my.task/update! :seon.task/proofs conj 'ns.x-test/a-proof)``, and run it red, ``(my.agent/prove)``.
- Then make it pass by redefining what it needs in its own namespace, keeping contracts, with a cost proof (``seon.cost/proof``) where input grows.
- A new helper is a ``defn-`` of its own, evaluated and tried at your REPL before anything calls it: its slips are found in its few lines.
- When a change needs a new value from a long loop, compute it in a helper from what the loop already answers, not by threading new state through the loop: each `recur` it passes through is one more place an edit must change at once.
- A short definition, evaluate it whole; a long one, replace the part that changes, ``(seon.repl/edit! 'ns/f "old text" "new text")``, and places that change together in one edit, ``[[old new] ...]`` (`changing-a-definition`, below).
- Check each change with the proof's own expression, the failing assertion your status shows, at your REPL, before you prove again.
- Never loosen a contract or a proof to make it pass.

## Designing a task

A design task turns a request, often a line or two, into the task the next agent implements: its plan, what the plan cites and its red proofs are that agent's whole starting context, so you build it for that agent (its instructions cite this section whole; the implement step reads your plan accumulated over its needs, and every citation in it is a part of its page).

- Find what the change touches. Start from the request's own words: ``(my.agent/help "a word of it")`` names the docs and functions it finds; read the functions with ``(clojure.repl/source ns/f)``, a namespace's every definition with ``(clojure.repl/source ns)``, and who calls one with ``(my.agent/callers 'ns/f)``. Reproduce what the request says is wrong in one eval; when the output is already right, the premise is false: fail the task with it, ``(my.task/fail! "why" evidence)``.
- Write as you learn: probe a function with the inputs your proof will assert, not every case it could meet, and once you can say what changes, write it into the plan, then the proof. A turn that only reads moves nothing.
- Cite what you find into the plan as you find it, at the depth the implement agent needs: three backticks for each function it changes and each it must call as it is (its source is shown), one backtick for a doc that explains the area, a bare name for one it only needs to know of. ``(my.task/update! :seon.task/plan str "\n\nWhat changes: ...")``, each function's name between three backticks. Each citation is a part of your own page too, so you read it as the implement agent will.
- Write the proofs that will show the change, first: each a deftest in its function's test namespace, its input built as its neighbours' (``(in-ns 'ns.x-test)``, then the deftest: no file), registered, ``(my.task/update! :seon.task/proofs conj 'ns.x-test/a-proof)``, and run red for the reason the plan gives, ``(my.agent/prove)``.
- Every change is a form at the REPL and its file is written by the merge, so a plan names forms, never files: a definition is its `defn` or ``(seon.repl/edit! 'ns/f ...)``, a schema ``(my.schema/register! k form)``, starting from ``(get (my.schema/forms) k)``, a doc ``(seon.repl/edit! "docs/x.md#anchor" ...)``.
- Then say in the plan what the implement step changes: each function and what it does then; a new function's defn with its contract and a stub body; each new schema registered; a cost proof (``seon.cost/proof``) for a function whose input grows.
- Confirm the plan is enough: read your page as the implement agent will see it, the plan and each part it cites. Whatever it would still have to search for, cite; whatever it cites that the change does not need, take out. You are done when an agent with only that page could make the change without exploring.
- Do not make the fix: the implement step makes it from your red proofs. Then end your turn with prose.

## From outside

A Claude session attached through the MCP server works the same task as a Seon agent does, with the server's tools in place of the one eval tool (`docs/server.md`; the tool list says each one's arguments). Where this page says "a reply" or "your turn", read the tool that does it:

- The agent you attached is yours: `seon_done`, `seon_eval` and `seon_message` on an agent another session attached are refused (`:seon.server/held`, naming its holder) unless you also name its task (`task`).
- `seon_attach` with the task (ns/name) starts your agent on the task's branch and answers its id; every other tool names that id. A task already worked answers its agent, with `:shared true`: whoever attached it shares it, and its `seon_done` ends it for both. With `fresh: true` it resets the task: a new branch from main and an empty conversation (the old branch stays). With `from: "a3"` instead of a task it forks agent a3: a clone of it, live values and all, on a branch of its head, working a sibling task `<name>-fork-<n>`; read its context first, then work it as any task, and cancel the one you do not want: root's my.root/cancel!, through `seon_eval` as root.
- `seon_context` is your turn: it answers what a model is sent now (the system prompt, your page and your conversation), runs your proofs and takes the messages sent to you. The first read shows the page's parts whole, each later one the parts that changed since the read before. Read it after every few evals, not after each.
- `seon_eval` evaluates Clojure in your agent: the forms in this page, `@ctx` and its `swap!`, your definitions. It is your REPL, one call per eval, answered as an eval is (`:handle :value :out :err :ex`). Pass the agent `root` for root's own REPL.
- `seon_prove` runs the tests your branch's change reaches, as the judge will; a result already green at its digest is not run again.
- `seon_message` tells an agent something; `seon_agents` lists the live agents and the tasks running or with runs, newest first, and counts the rest.
- `seon_done` ends your run: your proofs run, then root judges the run and merges it when the done rule refuses nothing. It answers the verdict, with the done rule's reasons when the task is held. There is no reply that ends a turn: finishing is `seon_done`, and a task that cannot be done as planned is failed with `my.task/fail!` through `seon_eval`, then handed back with `seon_done`, and root holds the task.

## What you read

Your context is the parts of your page and your conversation: the task's part, sent once and unchanged while you work, then how your work stands, which changes as your work does.

Your task's part:
- `:task`: its id, kind and instructions, and its plan as it accumulated along its needs, prerequisites first.
- `:to-prove` (only while one is not written): each proof not written yet and what the plan says it should assert. A written proof is the status's, with its verdict and source; write only those listed here (the steps: `working-a-task`, above).
- `:similar` (when the task turns it on): a few program functions nearest the plan in meaning that it does not name, a line each.

Each thing the plan cites is a part of its own, at the depth its delimiter asks (a function's doc, or its source with the form that changes part of it, and its source at doc depth too when it is of the namespaces your task works in: its own, its proofs' and what they call; a schema's form with the forms of the keys it names; a section's lead), and the namespaces your work touches are one part: in each, the names your task cites or its proofs call, and `:ns-form`, the form that reads its ns form (its requires and aliases) and a line per definition (``(clojure.repl/source ns)``). ``(my.task/data)`` answers the task's whole data, with its write set (each cited var's source and callers) and the sections it cites.
- Search first: ``(my.agent/search "text")`` finds the best matches over code and wiki sections (tests, tasks and schema keys when ``:kinds`` asks for them), fusing the text's exact words, its meaning and its references: ``[{:kind :name :score :by :relevance :line} ...]``, best first by ``:relevance``, the chance a hit is worth reading for your question (those under 40% are counted in the last entry, with the call that shows them: ``{:min 0.0}``), so you filter it with Clojure (``(filter #(= :section (:kind %)) (my.agent/search "rrf"))``); options focus it (``{:kinds #{:section :task} :by #{:text} :ns "seon.merge" :k 20}``). ``(my.agent/grep "text")`` is its text half over code alone, with no relevance cut: every exact hit, up to `:k`.
- More helpers the plan does not name: ``(my.agent/callees 'ns/f)``, ``(my.agent/callers 'ns/f)``, ``(my.agent/tests-reaching 'ns/f)`` (every test whose reach includes it, nearest first, with its last result), ``(my.agent/similar "text")`` (meaning alone), ``(clojure.repl/dir the.ns)``.

The conversation: each of your replies with its eval calls and their responses, and the messages sent to you, each `{:from :body}` (the owner's words, `:from "owner"`; root's; another agent's, its run id), where they arrived. Each is a fact (`:seon.message/*`) your turn writes on your branch when it takes it, another agent's send and root's request too.

How your work stands, one part (`status`; `(my.task/status)` answers the same), drawn from facts on your branch, so it is always now: it changes when your work does, never because a turn passed.
- `:proofs`: each of your proofs and its verdict, the one your last run recorded at the test's digest now. A red one shows each failure as `:assert` (as written) and `:got` (what it evaluated to), its source, and `:calls`, the program functions it calls, each by its name (``(clojure.repl/doc f)`` reads one's doc and contract). `:undefined` is not written yet; `:unknown` has no verdict at its digest (not run since it or what it calls changed): run `(my.agent/prove)`, never `clojure.test/run-test-var`, whose report goes to `*out*` and records nothing; `:not-run` means the test run failed (see `:test-run`); `:deferred` is a process test root's gate runs.
- `:refused`: what keeps you from done beyond your red proofs (the done rule, `seon.reconcile/done-explain`), each with the form that does something about it (`:do`): `:regression` (green on main, red here: your change broke it), `:stray-red` (a red test that is no task's proof; each red test shows its failures, its source and its `:calls`, as a red proof does: if you wrote it as one, its `:do` registers it, ``(my.task/update! :seon.task/proofs conj 'ns.x-test/a-proof)``; marked `:removed`, it is one of main's tests your branch removed, which still judges from main's definition), `:contract-gap` (a function you defined without a ``:malli/schema``), `:open-problem` (a problem your own work made true, such as a citation of a var you deleted), `:proof-unknown` (a proof with no verdict), `:unmerged` (a merge entry not resolved), `:no-proofs` (no proof registered yet, a design too), and on a design task `:no-design` (your plan is unchanged since the run began).
- `:test-run`: the test run itself failing (a dead fork, a namespace that did not load); no verdict is real until it runs.
- `:without-a-contract`, `:caused` and `:attention` (the wiki sections your code changes made stale, to read and probably fix, never a reason to refuse), `:its-merge-carries` (every var you added, changed and removed and every schema you changed: anything the task does not need, put back), `:unmerged` (the entries to resolve), and on a task that merges itself `:merge` (what main changed since your merge base, `:main-moved`, and which of those you changed too, `:conflicts`; once your proofs pass and nothing conflicts, your run ends when merging main brings nothing new, and root merges your branch).
- "Already green on main: …", first on your task's part: a written proof of yours whose last result on main is green (`seon.context/task-data`'s `:green-on-main`). The premise may already hold: reproduce it, and if the output is right, fail the task with that output in this turn.
- The run's part (`run`) shows your turn of your cap, the changes your evals made to your branch's program (`:changed`, each var defined, edited or unmapped, once: turn 11 of 40 · 0 changes means nothing has changed yet), `:ns`, the namespace your REPL is in (every eval's response carries it too, as nREPL's do: where your next eval runs), and, when it happened, that your last reply was cut at the model's token limit. A move into a namespace whose own var hides a REPL helper answers `:helper-shadowed`, naming both (`docs/render.md#a-part-answers-data`).
Your tests: `(my.agent/prove)` runs them; `(my.agent/deferred)` lists the process tests root runs when it asks.

## The REPL

This is a real Clojure REPL: what you define becomes part of your branch's program exactly as written.

- Your work is Clojure forms evaluated here. Your reply's text is recorded with the call's eval, and the owner reads it beside the code and its response.

- `(ns app.workout)` creates a namespace (with its `:require`s) and it becomes part of your program; ``(in-ns 'the.ns)`` then ``(defn f ...)`` changes an existing one, and its callers see it at once.
- Every function you define needs a Malli contract in its metadata: `(defn total {:malli/schema [:=> [:cat [:vector :int]] :int]} [xs] (reduce + 0 xs))`.
- Your own namespace is scratch and never merges: `(in-ns (symbol (str "agent." (name (:id (my.agent/whoami))))))`. There the `my.*` functions are referred unqualified; elsewhere call them qualified, as written here. The REPL's own helpers (`source`, `doc`, `dir`, `apropos`, `find-doc`, `pst`) follow you into any namespace you switch to, as `clojure.repl`'s do at a REPL.
- A schema changes as a function does, on your branch: `(my.schema/register! :app.workout/set [:map [:reps :int] [:kg :double]])`; your next forms see it. To change an existing key, start from its form: `(get (my.schema/forms) :seon.task/task)`.
- Your branch's program is the forms you evaluate, recorded on your branch; the files in the checkout are not your branch. Writing a file under src or test, or running git, changes the shared checkout under every agent and root, not your program: a file is an effect, through its owner.
- A top-level load-file of a string path evaluates the file's forms as top-level forms, each recorded (so `source` shows what runs); a load-file inside another form is Clojure's own and records nothing.
- An earlier response: ``result/e3``, or ``(my.agent/$ :e3)`` by its handle. Every response stays under its handle for the life of your process (your memory holds them). Keep a value you need later with a `def` or in `ctx`; the last value `*1`; the last error is `*e`, and ``(my.agent/pst)`` prints it with its causes.

## Small answers

Ask for the answer you need, never the whole value: a response is shown whole (nothing cuts your own results), so its size is the size of what you asked for. Look at a value's shape first, then narrow to the part you need. Each form below is the cheapest well-known Clojure for its question, measured on the program's 3,936 vars (ticks in millions).

- **How many** (`wc -l`): `(count (my.db/datoms :aevt :var/name))`, 0.3 M. A query that collects them first costs 1.3 M, and an aggregate ``(count ?e)`` 2.0 M.
- **The first few** (`head`): `(->> (my.db/datoms :aevt :var/name) (take 5) (map :v))` reads five datoms (0.003 M, whatever the db's size); ``(take 5 (my.db/q ...))`` computes the whole query first (1.3 M). When the answer needs a join, ask the query for the limit: `(datahike.api/q {:query '[:find ?n :where [_ :var/name ?n]] :args [(my.db/db)] :limit 5})` (0.02 M).
- **Matching lines** (`grep -n`): `(my.agent/grep "accumulated")`, every line holding it across the program's functions, tests, wiki sections, task plans and schema entries, a row each, ``{:at "seon.task/accumulate:3" :kind :var :text "..."}``. Narrow it with ``{:in #{:var :test} :ns "seon.task"}``, or as any list: `(->> (my.agent/grep "proofs" {:ns "seon.task"}) (take 5))`, `(count (my.agent/grep "proofs"))`. A string is matched as written and its words find the texts through the search index; a regex, `(my.agent/grep #"plan|proof")`, reads every text.
- **What a namespace or a page holds** (`ls`): `(my.agent/ls 'seon.task)`, each definition with its doc's first sentence; `(my.agent/ls "docs/merging.md")`, each section's heading.
- **Lines n to m** (`sed -n`): `(my.agent/lines 'seon.task/accumulate 3 6)`, numbered as grep numbers them, so a grep hit's `:at` says where to read; a section (``"docs/x.md#anchor"``), a page or a task's id the same way, and `(my.agent/lines 'seon.task/accumulate)` whole. The last few (`tail`): `(take-last 5 (my.agent/lines 'seon.task/accumulate))`.
- **How big, before you read it** (`wc`): `(my.agent/wc (my.agent/lines 'seon.task/accumulate))`, in tokens, what it would cost you printed.
- **Counts by kind** (`sort | uniq -c | sort -rn | head`): `(->> (my.db/datoms :aevt :var/name) (map (comp namespace :v)) frequencies (sort-by val >) (take 5))`.
- **The shape of a map** (`ls`, `du`): `(keys (my.task/current))`, then the size of each key's value, largest first: `(->> (update-vals (my.task/current) (comp count pr-str)) (sort-by val >) (take 5))`.
- **Some keys** (`cut`, `jq`): `(select-keys (my.task/current) [:seon.task/id :seon.task/proofs])`, ``(get-in m [:a :b])``, and for many maps ``(map #(select-keys % [:a :b]) ms)``. Pull the attributes you need, `(my.db/pull [:seon.task/id :seon.task/plan] [:seon.task/id (:seon.task/id (my.task/current))])`, rather than ``'[*]`` (an entity's `*` can be tens of KB: root's is 23 KB, most of it two keys).
- **A big value you will read in parts**: keep it, then read pieces by index: ``(def vs (vec (my.db/datoms :aevt :var/name)))``, then ``(count vs)`` and ``(subvec vs 0 10)``.

The ranking of these idioms is a cost proof (seon.repl-idioms-test): the first few by index cost the few, not the index.

## Changing a definition

A definition changes by evaluating its new text: whole, as any `defn`, or, for a long one, by replacing one exact piece of its recorded source with `seon.repl/edit!`. ``(seon.repl/edit! 'demo.sq/square "(* n n)" "(* n n n)")`` finds the old text in `(source demo.sq/square)`, which must hold it exactly once (whitespace counts), puts the new text in its place, and evaluates the whole new definition in its namespace through the one REPL path: it is recorded as program and its contract armed as a typed `defn` would be. It is read exactly as written, never repaired: new text that opens or closes delimiters the old text did not (`seon.reader/balance`, strings, character literals and comments aside) is refused, `:unbalanced`, saying which and by how many, since a repair of a whole definition is made far from the edit and reads as another error there. It answers that response with `:edited`, the var and the lines around the change (`:seon.config.agent/edit-context-lines` of context), so the change is seen without reading the definition again. It changes nothing when the old text is not there (``:seon.repl/edit-refused`` `:not-found`, with the lines holding its first line, indentation aside, and at each the one line where old stops matching, both texts and, when only indentation differs, each one's leading spaces: ``:seon.repl/differs``, `seon.repl/differs`), is there more than once (`:ambiguous`, each candidate's line: give more of it), or the var has no recorded source (`:no-source`). Its inner eval records no eval of its own (`:within`): the call that ran `edit!` is the one the conversation keeps.

A change that must reach several places at once is one edit: ``(seon.repl/edit! 'demo.sq/square [["old 1" "new 1"] ["old 2" "new 2"]])`` checks each pair as one alone is checked (exactly once, balanced; two whose old texts overlap are refused, `:overlap`), applies them together and evaluates the definition once. A loop that gains a binding needs its binding vector and each of its `recur`s changed together: edited one by one, each leaves a `recur` of the wrong arity and does not compile. An edit whose new definition does not compile or load is that eval's error, its message saying nothing changed, never a value that reads as done. Each says where: a refused edit names the delimiters its new text leaves open, closes with nothing open or pairs with another kind, by their line in the new text, and a failed one quotes the line of the new definition its error names (```seon.reader/delimiters```). A new helper is a `defn-` of its own, evaluated and tried at the REPL before the long definition calls it, so its slips are found in its few lines, not in the long one's.

Why exact text, once: the file editors this follows converged on it, a unique `old` string or a refused edit naming the candidates (v1's `seon.agent.fs/replace!`, clojure-mcp's `file_edit`, whose structural editors are on a path to deprecation because frontier models edit text reliably); a silent first match (clojure-mcp's `clojure_edit_replace_sexp`) changes the wrong place.

## Changing a doc

A doc (a heading of a page and its text, named by its address: the page's path, `#` and the heading's anchor) changes as a definition does: ``(seon.repl/edit! "docs/agent.md#ending" "old text" "new text")`` replaces the exact old text, found once in the doc's block (its heading line, a blank line, its text, as `(seon.doc/doc-source (seon.agent/db) "docs/agent.md#ending")` shows it), and loads its page again on your branch, so its lead, the names it cites and what is stale follow the new text; several changes are one edit as for a definition, and its parentheses are prose, never counted. A cited doc's part names the form that changes it (`:change`). The change is on your branch's store, never a file; the page is drawn back from the store byte for byte (```seon.doc/markdown```). It merges as your code does, and its page is written back to its file with the merge.

## ctx

`@ctx` is your record as one map (`ctx` is referred into your own namespace only; from any other it is `seon.ctx/ctx`, read as `@seon.ctx/ctx` and changed by `swap!` the same way): your agent entity pulled by a pattern (its own attributes, your task `:seon.agent/task` with the vars it cites and your proofs, each with its last result as the `:seon.test/*` facts your branch holds, every turn and eval of yours with no window, and your newest messages; runs and catch-ups are not in it), with `:t`, `:branch` and `:commit` beside them (``seon.ctx/ctx``, one atom in your process). It is kept current by a patch from each transaction you make, so a read is a deref and builds nothing; `(keys @ctx)` lists what it holds.

- To see more or less of it, change its pattern: Datahike's pull syntax, held as data at `:seon.ctx/pattern`. `(:seon.ctx/pattern @ctx)` is your own edit (nil until you make one), and ``(swap! ctx assoc :seon.ctx/pattern [...])`` writes it; the record is built again by it (`(my.agent/help "docs/ctx.md#the-pattern")`). Your evals and turns are all there (no window); your messages are the newest few, and a printed cut names the form that reads the rest. What your work only measures (a turn's model call and its tokens, your process's pid and memory, an embedding) is on its own entity pointing at what it describes, never in `@ctx` (docs/ctx.md#the-record-is-the-agents-work).
- To change something, `swap!` it as any atom, with no query to write: a collection (your turns, your messages, your evals) is keyed by each member's id, newest first, and your task is `(:task @ctx)`:
  - your task's plan: ``(swap! ctx assoc-in [:task :seon.task/plan] "...")``, or ``(swap! ctx update-in [:task :seon.task/plan] str "\n\nFound: ...")``;
  - its proofs, or what it cites: ``(swap! ctx update-in [:task :seon.task/proofs] conj 'app.x-test/t1)``;
  - a member gone: ``(swap! ctx update :evals dissoc eid)``; a new one, its identity in its value: ``(swap! ctx assoc-in [:messages "m-note"] {:seon.message/id "m-note" :seon.message/from "agent" :seon.message/body "..."})``;
  - your own datum: register its key, then ``(swap! ctx assoc :app.x/notes [...])``;
  - what your page searches for: a search is a member like any other, its query searched on your branch at each draw and its hits shown as a part of your page (a function's name and first sentence, a section's heading). Your task's request has one, under ``(:searches (:task @ctx))``; change its words by a `swap!` that puts new words at `[:task :searches e :seon.search/query]`, e its key in that map, and add your own beside it: `(swap! ctx assoc-in [:searches "a1/where-entries-are-read"] {:seon.search/id "a1/where-entries-are-read" :seon.search/query "entries an agent has not taken" :seon.search/k 5})`. Only that part is drawn again, and your next turn is sent what changed of it;
  - what you read beside your task: ``(my.agent/cite! "docs/flow.md?full#idioms")`` and ``(my.agent/uncite! "docs/flow.md#idioms")`` (a page whole with no anchor; a name too);
  - a var the task cites is changed by its definition, as at any REPL: ``(in-ns 'app.x)`` then ``(defn f0 ...)``, and its cite's `:var` (under `[:task :cites]`) holds the new source at the next deref (a `swap!` of it is refused, naming that form).

  The change is one transaction on your branch, each value checked by its schema, or it is refused with the error that says why and nothing is written: ``:seon.ctx/invalid`` (no schema, or a value its schema refuses), ``:seon.ctx/not-held`` (the db would not hold it), ``:seon.ctx/basis`` (the swap!'s function wrote the db itself, or wrote `:t`, `:branch` or `:commit`, which are read-only). A refused swap! is never retried.
- What arrives each turn: your first request shows your page's parts whole; each later turn adds, at its place in your conversation, the parts whose drawing changed since your last request, `{part-id hiccup}` (nil for a part that left), each part sent once for each change (``seon.context/request``, docs/render.md#the-page-is-sent-as-what-changed). Your own evals and replies are your conversation, never repeated in a part. An eval runs once, when you send it: nothing is evaluated again for you.
- Attached from outside, each `seon_context` read is a turn the same way (``seon.context/request``): the first shows the page's parts whole, each later one the parts that changed since the read before.

## Finding your way

The program as your branch records it answers where things are, so read before you write.

- By name: `(clojure.repl/apropos "accumulate")`; by docstring: `(clojure.repl/find-doc "accumulated plan")`; by its text: `(my.agent/grep "accumulated")`, every line holding it (a name holding it, then a docstring, then a body), each with its matching line.
- A function: `(clojure.repl/doc seon.task/accumulate)` and `(clojure.repl/source seon.task/accumulate)`; who calls it: `(my.agent/callers 'seon.task/accumulate)`; every test that reaches it, nearest first, with its last result: `(my.agent/tests-reaching 'seon.task/accumulate)`; what it calls: `(my.agent/callees 'seon.task/accumulate)`.
- A namespace's ns form, its requires and aliases (to write a test where its siblings are), then every definition, privates included, a line each with its doc's first sentence: `(clojure.repl/source seon.task)` (```my.agent/source-fn```). Which namespaces require it, and which it requires (whether a new require would close a cycle): `(my.agent/callers 'seon.task)` and `(my.agent/callees 'seon.task)`. A name that is no var answers ```my.agent/no-var```, the one answer for a missing name that `source`, `callers` and `callees` give: the nearest existing names by Jolt's did-you-mean ranking (the namespace's definitions, or the nearest namespaces when it names none), the registry key of that name if there is one, the form that lists the namespace, and "write it" only when your task cites the name.
- A schema: its form, `(my.schema/forms :seon.task/task)`; every key, `(keys (my.schema/forms))`. It changes at the REPL, ``(my.schema/register! k form)``: the file it merges into is root's.
- A namespace, every definition, privates too, in file order, a line each (its name and its doc's first sentence): `(my.agent/help 'seon.print.select)`; read the few you need whole with `source`. Its publics alone: `(clojure.repl/dir my.task)`; every program namespace: `(my.db/q '[:find [?n ...] :where [_ :ns/name ?n]])`.
- Where an error is raised: its `:seon.error/operation` names the function that built it; the text that builds one, `(my.agent/grep "insufficient-floor-budget")` (its `:seon.error/type`'s name).
- Your task: `(my.task/current)`; one task by id: `(seon.task/task (my.db/db) (:seon.task/id (my.task/current)))`.
- An effect (a file, a model, git) goes through its owner: ``(my.fx/call :kind req)``.

## Testing

A test runs on the world: `my.test`'s small db shaped like production, plus what the test adds, under a ceiling of work every test is held to. How, with examples: `docs/testing.md#testing-in-the-world`.

- The default: ``(my.test/with (my.test/world) (my.test/code 'app.x "(ns app.x)\n(defn f [x] (inc x))\n"))``, a db value; add a page (``my.test/page``), a task (``my.test/task``), take a var out (``my.test/without``), or pass any tx-data or ``(fn [db] tx-data)``.
- A claim about how work grows is a cost proof (``seon.cost/proof``, small sizes), never a ratio of two readings; ``(my.test/cost #(+ 1 2))`` shows one call's ticks and bytes at the REPL.
- ``(my.agent/prove)`` runs the tests your change reaches and answers each verdict and its cost.

## Done

Your task is done when every proof passes and the done rule refuses nothing; the proofs run after each of your replies. A run that ends refused (a red or unrun proof, a reason, no progress, a dead agent, a refused merge) is not retried: its task is held for root, which decides whether to run it again (`docs/tasks.md#a-refused-run-is-held-for-root`).

- A proof is a deftest in a test namespace: `(ns app.workout-test (:require [clojure.test :refer [deftest is]] [app.workout :as w]))`, then `(deftest total-sums (is (= 6 (w/total [1 2 3]))))`, run with `(clojure.test/run-tests 'app.workout-test)`.
- A cost proof (`seon.cost/proof`) is your performance goal: it says what N counts, the least work the data needs (`:work`, e.g. one pass with one lookup per row), how that grows and what it may cost. Read it before writing code and do no more work than it states.
- Run what judges your branch now, as root's judge does: ``(my.agent/prove)`` runs your proofs and every test your change reaches, reusing each one green at its reach digest, and answers one line: its verdict and how many ran, were left to the judge (process tests), were reused and died (`my.agent/prove-summary`). What it found is drawn on that eval, from its value (`seon.render.ui/proved`: each proof's status, each red test with its first failing assertion, the tests whose child died, not counted); the whole run (`:results`, `:selected`, `:deferred`, `:why`, `:reports`) is the value under its handle. ``(my.agent/prove '#{ns.x-test/a-test})`` runs those tests alone, whatever your branch changed (a namespace's name, every test of it). A prove naming its tests counts like a bare one: `(my.task/status)` and the judge read each result by its test's digest. A process test (one whose reach holds a process var: it starts an agent, a root, a fork or the binary) never runs there: it is under `:deferred`, and `:why` names the process var it reaches and the calls to it. A process test that is your proof runs at the judge, after `seon_done`, before the merge; to see it red or green first, run its namespace by name (`seon_prove` with `names`).
- Each run of your tests also records its results as `:seon.test/*` facts on your branch (`seon.agent/record-results!`, `seon.proofs/result-tx`), which `@ctx` holds under your task's proofs and the task part draws; `bin/test` and `bin/prove` keep the shared verdict cache, so a test green at its digest for anyone is reused by you (docs/merging.md#verdicts-are-a-cache).
- The judge counts exactly (`seon.cost/slack`): allocated bytes may reach 1.25x the estimate, contracted calls 1.0x and ticks (the counted time dimension) 3x. CPU time (`:ns`) is measured and shown ("TIME OVER") but never red. A dimension whose growth exponent exceeds `:growth`'s by more than `seon.cost/steeper-by` (0.3) is STEEPER (an extra pass), red even inside the slack; LOOSE (a third of the estimate or less) is not red. Its failure message prints measured over estimate per size.

An agent's run records no `:ms` (```seon.proofs/answer-ran``` carries none), so ```seon.fx/typical-ms``` knows only `bin/test`'s durations. The judge's test selection follows reach: a print-only change to five keys selected 991 tests and ran 363, because `seon.print.select` is the printer core every test reaches.

## Merging

Main's work is merged into your branch as main moves (a work agent's too), and a merge task's branch has main merged in: each entry both changed is unmerged, shown under your status's `:unmerged` with its `:base`, `:ours` and `:theirs`, and resolved by you at the REPL.

- `(my.branch/unmerged)` lists them. Resolve one by redefining the var with the content both sides need, or keep one side: ``(my.branch/resolve! 'ns/var :theirs)``, or ``(my.branch/resolve! 'ns/var)`` to keep yours.
- Your branch sees only its own changes until then.

## Ending

Record what you learned in the plan, so the next step starts from it, then stop.

- `(my.task/update! :seon.task/plan str "\n\nFound: seon.task/accumulate owns the plan's accumulation.")`.
- When the task cannot be done as planned, fail it with the form that shows why: `(my.task/fail! "the premise is refuted" '(seon.task/accumulate {} "x"))`. The failure goes on the task, your run ends, and the task is held for root (`docs/tasks.md#a-refused-run-is-held-for-root`).
- `(my.turn/wait)` ends your turn and waits for root.
