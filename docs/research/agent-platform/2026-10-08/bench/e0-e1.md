### Verdict per task (decision rule: green shares differ by at least 2 of 5, or the CtG ranges do not overlap)

- **T1 (one contracted function): no difference.** At 5 runs both cells were 5/5 and the ranges overlapped. At 10 runs
  it was 10/10 against 8/10, with ranges [1-6] and [1-4]. Both typed failures were one-call runs that ended on a
  no-form reply (finding F2), so they say nothing about wording.
- **T2 (register a schema, then a function using it): typed wins.** 4/5 against 1/5. The typed intent names
  `seon.schema/register!` and `seon.schema/schema-definition`. The prose intent names neither, and its agents spent
  their calls looking for the registration function (finding F4).
- **T3 (write a passing deftest): typed wins.** 5/5 against 2/5. The two prose failures that hit the cap read the
  prose's "write a test named slug-lowercases" without the namespace-qualified target. They then spent calls on
  `my.program` plumbing (F3) and on one platform refusal of a correct deftest (F6).
- **T4 (two namespaces, an alias): leans typed, under the threshold.** At 5 runs it was 3/5 against 4/5. At 10 runs it
  was 4/10 against 7/10, with overlapping ranges: a difference of 1.5 per 5, inside the measured E0 noise. Four of the
  ten prose failures were runs lost to a no-form reply (F2), not to wording.

**Decision for the next experiments: typed wording becomes the task default.** It wins two tasks clearly, and loses
none. Its cost is about 1.5× the prompt tokens on T2 and T4, because typed runs reach more calls with larger exchanges.
At about US$0.0015 per run this does not matter.

**Caveat on the denominators.** The cap of 6 calls binds in 37 of 65 runs. Two platform behaviours (F1, F2) remove
about a third of all calls from useful work, and they hit both wordings. They add noise that the wording comparison
cannot remove. Fixing F1 and F2 before E2 should narrow the noise band.

## What the runs showed about context

Classes: **A** means what the context says (help, block, task wording, rendered values); **B** means platform
behaviour the agent cannot work around (turn loop, writer, evaluator); **C** means model behaviour that Seon can only
steer.

**F1 (B, with an A part): the turn loop runs past green proofs.** Of 322 calls, 97 (30%) came after the run's proofs
were green, and 26 of 45 green runs hit the cap. The help says "Green proofs finish the task", but
`seon.turn/continuing-reply?` continues while each reply evaluates a form. The agents, having no taught way to stop,
invent one. Run `area-is-contracted-typed-a9752a-2` went green on call 1 and then sent `:seon.context/complete` (a
keyword that exists nowhere in Seon) for five calls until the cap. Filed as task
`seon.turn/the-turn-loop-stops-on-prose-and-runs-past-green`, together with F2.

**F2 (B): one reply with no form ends the run for good.** 9 of 65 runs ended unfinished after 1-3 calls because a reply
evaluated no form, and nothing woke them again. The loop's rule waits on such a reply, and no further wake arrives. Two
shapes:
- The model writes its forms commented out. An example is run `area-is-contracted-typed-f6a162-3`, which wrote
  `;; (in-ns 'seon.competency)` and `;; (doc …)`. The help's "write every sentence as a ;; comment" invites this
  (class A).
- The model writes one prose word. Run `app-calls-util-through-an-alias-prose-f6a162-1`'s second reply was "Assess".

Filed with F1.

**F3 (A): `dir` shows internal plumbing and supplied arguments.** `(dir my.program)` lists `my.program/supplied-context`
(`:seon.fn/internal? true`), and it shows arglists with supplied members such as `my.program/source`'s `[database sym]`.
Agents then pass them by hand:
- `(my.program/source (my.program/supplied-context {}) 'comp.geo/area)` and `(my.program/diff
  (my.program/supplied-context nil))` gave 8 refusals across the runs.
- `(my.program/source 'comp.util)` with a namespace symbol gave 12 refusals: agents expect `source` of a namespace to
  answer.

Filed as task `seon.sci.lookup/dir-shows-internal-functions-and-supplied-arguments`.

**F4 (A): nothing teaches how to register a schema.** Every T2 prose run looked for the registration function with `dir
seon.schema` (a listing of about 100 entries), `doc` of unrelated functions and regex filters over `ns-publics`.
`quantity-schema-then-function-prose-76167b-4` spent five calls this way before finding `seon.schema/register!` on
the sixth. The help names no schema registration form. Filed as task `seon.bootstrap/help-teaches-no-schema-registration`.

**F5 (B): a quoted alias refuses every program fact of its eval request.**
- In `app-calls-util-through-an-alias-prose-35d2a9-1`, one eval request held `(ns comp.app (:require [comp.util :as
  u]))`, `(defn bounded …)` and `(require '[comp.util :as 'u])`.
- SCI accepted the last form and recorded the alias local as the sequence `(quote u)`. The writer refused
  `:seon.ns.alias/local` ("expected a symbol, got a sequence").
- Both the correct `ns` form and the `defn` were answered "Not admitted to the program", although at the REPL `(mapv
  comp.app/bounded [15 -3 7])` answered `[10 0 7]`.
- Clojure refuses `:as 'u` at the form.

Filed as task `seon.sci.eval/a-quoted-alias-refuses-the-whole-requests-program-facts`.

**F6 (B): a correct deftest was refused by the digest check.** In `slug-has-a-passing-test-prose-35d2a9-2`, one eval
request held `(require '[clojure.test :refer [deftest is]])` and then `(deftest slug-lowercases (is (= "hello-world"
(ct/slug "Hello World"))) …)`. The deftest was answered "The stored definition digest of [:seon.test/sym
comp.text-test/slug-lowercases] is not the one its definition produces". The agent then defined the test through
`eval` (it ran 2 passes at the REPL), but the proof never saw an admitted declaration. This resembles the alias-retarget
class (`seon.db/the-digest-refusal-names-no-cause`, `seon.db/a-cross-pass-alias-retarget-leaves-dependant-digests-stale`),
but here a refer was added rather than retargeted. Filed as task
`seon.db/a-deftest-after-a-same-request-refer-is-refused-by-digest`.

**F7 (B): a refusal of a database-value argument cannot be built.** An agent called `(seon.db/transact! (seon.db/db)
[…])`. JVM repro: `(seon.db/transact! (seon.db/db (seon.cluster.boot/connection "default")) [{:seon.ns/sym
'scratch.bench/none}])` answers "the refusal could not be built: class datahike.datom.Datom cannot be cast to class
java.util.Map$Entry". The refusal builder walks the database value as a map. Filed as task
`seon.instrument/a-refusal-of-a-database-argument-cannot-be-built`.

**F8 (C, steered by the transcript format): the model imitates the transcript.**
- 44 of 322 replies begin a line with `user=>`, the prompt marker shown in the exchanges.
- Several replies stop after one form at the `#:seon.repl{` stop sequence, as the model starts writing the reply it
  expects.
- Prose words evaluated as symbols ("Let", "the", "how": about 20 runs show one) are the existing
  `seon.turn/a-prose-only-reply-evaluates-its-first-word`.

E2 (wire format, CX-12) tests this directly. No new task.

**F9 (A, small): agents call the proof test as a function.** `(seon.competency-test/t1-area-is-contracted)` answers
"Unable to resolve symbol", because the test namespace is not loaded in the agent's SCI context. The block names the
proof but gives no way to run it. Seen in 3 runs. The agent proceeds once the next reply shows the proof result, so
this is not filed separately. It belongs with the help-and-block wording of F1.

**Grade note.** `grade` counts the contract refusals an agent provokes on purpose, such as `(comp.geo/area -1 3)` in
T1, as agent errors (6 of the 36 argument refusals). The agent-error column therefore overstates mistakes on contract
tasks. A per-error-type column needs the errors cut (`:seon.cluster.eval/error-type`, spec §6).

### New findings

- **G1 (tool; not yet filed as a task): `launch!` waits until the deadline when a run ends `:done`.** `seon.competency/run-state`
  decides `:waiting` from `seon.turn/continuing-reply?` and does not read the task's new `:done` state, so 15 of 20

  about 4.5 minutes of wall time against 44-67 s in E1. The grades are right (the calls and CtG come from the
  database), but `terminal` reads `:deadline` for green runs. Fix: `run-state` returns `:waiting` once the task is
  `:done`.
- **G2: the MCP time limit.** The launch ran in a `future`, polled with `deref` and a timeout, and finished
  without a launch refusal, memory gate refusal or fault.
- T3 prose-reply failure and the two T4 capped runs are model errors on the task, as in E1; no new platform error

### What the runs showed

- **CX-4 removed the copied prompt.** No reply in either format holds a `<ns>=>` line (0 of 244, against 44 of 322 in
  E1), and no reply holds `#:seon.repl`. F8's prompt imitation is gone in both formats, so it was the printed

- **H1 (B, platform; existing task): prose evaluated word by word.** Run
