# Render: what a person or a model reads

Every view, the model's context included, is a render: the one value printer (`seon.print`), or a render function declared on the value's schema (``:seon.render/html`` for the page, ``:seon.render/ai`` for the context). Nothing else shapes output, and nothing clips.

**It's like React, or Reagent.** A render is a component: a plain function of a value and its props, `(fn [value opts] result)` (owner, 2026-10-23: "It's all just functions and results"). Its props are optional: `:as`, the room it is shown in (`:inline`, one line in a chat stream or a table cell; `:block`, a full panel, the default; `:tile`, compact and live; `:row`, a row of a page's list), `:budget`, its tokens, and `:handle`, the form its cuts read the rest through. A component that does not care about a prop ignores it. Its result is hiccup for the page (`:html`) or text for the model (`:ai`, `{:text :truncated?}`). A schema declares the component of its values, one per face (`{:seon.render/html sym :seon.render/ai sym}`), and `seon.render/render` finds it in O(1) by the value's schema (the producer's `:schema`, the value's metadata, an error map's type, or an identity attribute: `seon.render/schema-of`); a value with none takes the default component, the one printer (`seon.render/print-html`, `print-ai`), which takes the same props and auto-renders: wherever a value inside the printed one has a render of its own, the printer shows that render in its place, one line (`seon.print/print-value`'s `:render`: the nested value's `:ai` render in the text, a call of its `:html` render in the hiccup), in its one pass (`seon.render-cost-test/the-printer-renders-nested-values-in-one-pass`: 19 contracted calls a nested value, linear). Inside hiccup, a vector headed by a function is a component call, as in Reagent: `[task-row task {:as :inline}]` is written as what `(task-row task {:as :inline})` answers, itself expanded the same way, by the one serializer (`seon.web.html/html`); a keyword head is an HTML element. The render proc is the reconciliation, one level up: a page's parts (its units) render again only when their input value changed, and only the bytes that changed are sent (`seon.render-cost-test/render-calls-are-one-pass-over-their-nodes`: nested calls serialize in one pass, linear in their nodes).

## No clipping

A cut never drops silently. It says what it omitted and how to read the rest. Storage keeps the exact value (an error's message, a running form, a provider's HTTP body travel whole); renders budget it. There is no `clip`, no `subs` for display, no `take n` without its count, and no stylesheet clip (`text-overflow: ellipsis`, `line-clamp`, `overflow: hidden` on text): the render's budget is the clamp.


## Prose, and the one string cutter

Text is cut one way, inside a value or shown as itself: the printer's string cutter (`seon.print.select/break-at`) ends the shown part after a line break in its room's second half, else after a space in its final quarter, else at the room, and the cut says `…+N chars in M more lines` and the form that reads exactly the rest (`seon.print.emit/cut-words`). A string in a printed value reads `"its prefix"…+7279 chars in 88 more lines (subs result/h3 19523)`. `seon.print/prose` shows text as itself (unquoted) under a token budget, sized as the printer sizes (`:seon.config.ai/chars-per-token-prior` characters a token, `seon.print.select/estimate`, the one sizer: the history budget and the scripted model's usage count with it too): whole when it fits, else that same cut; given a source form that answers the whole text, the cut names `(subs source offset)` (with `:one-line?`, the offset is still the source's). On the page, `seon.render.ui/prose` links the cut to the page that shows the whole, or carries the whole in the cut's title; a printed value's cut names its result handle (`result/h3 at its REPL`).

A cut is read as its text, where it stands: the printer answers no list of its cuts. Decided by blind scoring on production-shaped values (`notes/printer-eval/README.md`): Haiku readers answered every question about a cut value from the inline cut alone (100%, 60 answers), and ranked it first in 30 of 30 three-way comparisons against the same text with a closing "left out" list (repeats every cut, up to 2.9x the tokens) or with footnote markers; prose's cut words beat the bare `…+N` in 18 of 22 pairs (the unit and the line count tell a reader how much it is missing), and prose's break beat a cut at the last line end, which can spend most of the room on nothing (47 of 256 tokens shown in one case).


## A render is a value, printed once

A view that chooses what to show (`seon.render.ai`'s renders: an error, a task, a run, a problem row, main's problems, root's context) makes a value, never text: an array map of what matters, in its order (`seon.render.ai/task-data`, `run-data`, `error-data`, `problem-data`, `report-data`, `system-data`), and the one printer prints it once at the reader's budget. Nothing is cut before the printer: a long list or a long plan is the printer's cut, which says what it left and reads its rest through the form that makes the value again (`(get-in (seon.render.ai/task-data result/h3) [:plan])`). Where a list's whole lives elsewhere, the value carries the form that reads it as data (`:all-in (seon.task/runs (deref (:conn sys)))`). Root's context groups what repeats: blocked tasks by what blocks them (`seon.render.ui/waiting-on`, the page's own). It replaces the bounded lists (a list cut to a config key's first items with a `+N more` line, deleted with their keys) and the per-field budgets that cut each part before the printer (clipping audit, 2026-10-25).


A render's budget, when its env names none, is config too: `:seon.config.render/value-budget` for a value and `:seon.config.render/root-context-budget` for root's context (`seon.render.ai/system`).

## The walk

The walk is the default view of any entity: the entity, the neighbours its schema declares one hop away, and the units of each neighbour its schema names a part one hop more (owner, 2026-10-23: "locations should be based on a starting EID and we pull from there to render the data what one hop away"). An entity whose schema declares an `:html` view of its own shows by that; every other shows by the walk (root, 2026-10-24: keep the walk's core as the default view).

- **Units are declared on the entity's schema:** `:seon.render/units` on its entity schema (`{:seon.db/attributes true}`), attributes in pull's own syntax, a leading `_` the reverse ref. An agent (`:seon.db/agent-fact`) shows its runs and its evaluations; a run (`:seon.run/run-fact`) its task, its turns and the messages to it; a task (`:seon.task/task-fact`) its plan and proofs, its own values; root (`:seon.root/root-fact`) its agents. `:seon.render/parts` names the units whose own units join the page (an agent's run: so an agent's page lists its run's turns and messages). An entity's schema is the entity schema naming its identity attribute; a neighbour with none (an evaluation) takes the one naming the ref that reached it (```seon.render/entity-schema```). `:seon.render/keys` names the attributes a view of it shows (`relevant`).
- **```seon.render/walk```** takes the dbs a page reads, main first, and answers `{:seon.render/root :seon.render/nodes}`. A **node** is an entity (`:seon.render/kind :entity`, its relevant attributes, its schema, its eid) or one of the walked entity's own values (`:value`, a task's plan). Each says where it came from (`:seon.render/from`), the db it was read from (`:seon.render/db`, the first holding it: what root observed of an agent on main wins over its branch's older copy), and, for a neighbour, the transaction of the datom that linked it (`:seon.render/tx`): nodes come newest first by it, since transactions are issued store-wide and an id is not (a content id is a hash). An eid no db holds answers `:seon.render/no-entity`.
- Cost: three contracted calls a neighbour, its linking datom and its pull (`seon.render-cost-test/a-walk-reads-its-entity-and-one-hop`: 308 / 3,008 at 100 / 1,000 evaluations).

What a view does not need is cut at its producer or given a focused render on its schema, never filtered by the view. Root's open problems hold no ref to root (recorded errors are keyed by signature), so root's page adds them as a part of its own ([the page](#the-page)).

## The page

Every page is the walk of one entity, laid out by one layout, ```seon.render.ui/page``` (owner, 2026-10-23: "no separate run view it's part of the agents view"; "root is also an agents view, it just has different focus").

- **A location is an entity.** `/` is root's entity on main, `/agent/{id}` the agent of that id in root's boot (its key, `seon.task/agent-key`: ids start again each boot), and `/walk/{eid}` any entity (`seon.render.proc/location`). Every tile links to its entity's page.
- **The list.** A unit whose entity schema says `{:seon.render/as :row}` is a row of the page's one list, in the walk's order (```seon.render.ui/rows```): an evaluation (`:seon.db/eval-fact`, by ```seon.render.ui/evaluation```), a model turn (`:seon.turn/turn-fact`, ```seon.render.ui/turn```) with the evaluations its calls name under it, a message. Each evaluation is in one row only.
- **The input is at the top**: an agent's REPL, messages, interrupt and release (```seon.render.ui/agent-input```), root's spawn and evaluate-in-every-agent (```seon.render.ui/root-input```), each a UI call of root's request table ([dashboard](dashboard.md#callbacks-are-data)).
- **The tiles.** Every other unit is a tile beside the list: an agent's run, its task; root's agents. Root's focus adds main's open problems (`seon.problems/health`) as a part of its page, by their own schema's render.
- **Only what is on screen is rendered.** A page renders its newest `:seon.config.render.list/history` rows; its end (```seon.render.ui/more```) counts the older ones and, when it scrolls into view, posts `/more` (Datastar's `data-on-intersect`), and the render proc renders as many more (an event, never a poll).

## The page is components

A page's layout answers its skeleton, with a slot where each part goes, and `:seon.render/units`. Each entity part is a **component**, `{:comp [db eid as]}`: the entity read from its db and drawn by its schema's `:html` render at a size (```seon.render.ui/component```: the main pane, a row, a tile; a turn on the debug page). The render proc renders a component once, inside `d/with-reads`, into one cache shared by every page (keyed by its db, eid and size), and indexes what it read; a commit's datoms (main's from the writer, a branch's by its datoms-diff) find the components they touched (```seon.render.proc/comps-touched```), which render again, and only the pages holding one of them pass (```seon.render.proc/invalidate```). A value part (a task's plan, root's open problems, a branch's read error) is `{:input :schema}` and renders when its value changes. A component no page holds leaves the cache. Cost: a commit renders the components it touched, whatever the page's rows (`seon.render-cost-test/a-transaction-renders-what-it-touched`); a page shown on two locations renders each component once (`seon.render-test/a-component-on-two-pages-renders-once`). Each part is drawn by its `:html` render alone: its `:ai` face is the model's and is never made for the page (`seon.render-test/a-page-part-renders-only-its-view`).

The frame is the one part with no value behind it: the document (`seon.web/page-doc`), its head and scripts (`seon.render.ui/shell`). It is fixed hiccup, the same on every page, served once with the page.

## Tailwind

The page is laid out by Tailwind's classes in each render's hiccup, responsive at every width, with no hand-written stylesheet (owner, 2026-10-23: "I want responsive html and css so why not tailwind?"). `resources/public/css/input.css` holds every directive, token and source glob (Tailwind v4, CSS-first, no config file): `source(none)` with an explicit `@source` over `src/**/*.clj` (automatic detection walked the reference-code tree: v1 measured 42 s against 1 s), the Phosphor theme's tokens (dark, light when the system asks), a base layer for semantic hiccup and the one printer's classes, and a safelist of the utilities an agent may use, since a render an agent defines at its REPL is in no scanned file. `bin/build css` (a step of `bin/build seon`, and so of the `:build` effect after merges) runs Tailwind's standalone binary, pinned by version and checksum and fetched once into `target/tailwind/`, into `target/css/app.css` (`:seon.config.web/stylesheet`, served at `/app.css` and read per request); its stamp is the digest of `input.css` and the scanned sources, so unchanged inputs regenerate nothing (68 ms when they changed, v1's port of its `bin/css`). Add a class to a render, or a token or safelist entry to `input.css`, and build: the stylesheet regenerates.

## Tiles are data; the context reads the page's derivations

Each fact the page and root's context both show has one producer they share:
- **A lifecycle is declared once, on its enum's schema,** and read through one reader, `seon.contract/properties` (an enum's members, in order: `seon.contract/enum-values`):
  - `:seon.agent/status` carries its live, stopped and gone sets (`seon.root/gone-statuses`, read by root, the web, the server and the check harness; `seon.render.ui/live`; `seon.render.ui/status-order`);
  - `:seon.run/status` its finished, judged and failed sets, read once, by `seon.task` (`seon.task/finished`, `judged`, `failed`; with `seon.task/done?`, a task's done on a db, and `seon.task/running`, the running runs by one index seek), which the reconciler, problems, the render UI and the server require (`seon.main-test/a-runs-lifecycle-is-read-once`);
  - `:seon.task/state` a task's observed states with each pill's title (`seon.render.ui/state-title`). A task's state is derived once, from the reconciler's index (`seon.reconcile/task-state`), and every task row reads it (`seon.render.proc/task-rows`).

  A new status is one enum entry. Not this: a status set typed out at each site that asks.
- **How an agent is:** `seon.render.ui/shown-status` and `seon.render.ui/ended-as`; its newest events are the stream's own words (`seon.render.ui/event-detail`); a cost verdict's lines are `seon.cost/verdict-lines`. The writer, the fork server and main are `seon.render.ui/load-tiles`, data root's context prints one line each.
- **Runs, merges and evaluations:** a test run's words are `seon.render.ui/run-words` and its redness `seon.proofs/red?`; a merge is its run's end (`:seon.run/status` `:merged`, shown on its task's row); an evaluation is one render, ```seon.render.ui/evaluation```, for its entity, its record and a reply item.
- **Errors:** an error or failure shown reads `:seon.error/message` of the one error map ([errors](errors.md#errors)), never a second `{:error "text"}` shape.
- **Numbers:** root's view numbers (p50s, transactions per commit) are `seon.root/view-numbers`, read by `seon.root/state` and the render proc alike.
- One-line text in a row or the context is `seon.print/line`. A root function called from the page is recorded on main by the render proc when root answers it (origin `:ui`), with that answer ([dashboard](dashboard.md)).


## Health is what flows

Nothing is pinged and nothing runs on a beat. A render pass happens on an event, an api answer or a tab's request. What is wrong has one producer, what is recorded or derived from main's facts: root's context (`seon.render.ai/problems`) and root's page read root's defects and main's open problems (`seon.problems/health`: [health](health.md)), never re-derive them from the live view; root records an agent's death (`seon.root/end-agent`), and a refused spawn, or a red test run or refused merge of an agent with no task (a task's run records its own), as it folds it into the agent's view (`seon.root/set-agent`). A value only a ping carried is its owner's to emit when it changes. `bin/check idle` proves an idle root with a tab open steps nothing, and that a spawn and a commit still reach the tab.


## The model's context: a cached prefix, then the turn

A model request sends what does not change first, so the provider serves it from its prefix cache, and sends nothing twice.

The request is a render of the agent's record (notes/design-agent-view-2026-10-24.md §3, owner 2026-10-24): each record's `:seon.render/ai` renders its own part of the conversation as provider-neutral messages (an eval, ```seon.context/eval-messages```: its assistant message with the reply's prose, its reasoning as returned and the eval call, then its tool message, the response as printed; anyone else's eval, one user message; a message fact, ```seon.context/message-messages```), and ```seon.context/request``` composes the agent's request from them in one order; the one provider format function, ```seon.model/wire-messages```, turns it into the wire shape (a reply's calls folded into one assistant message; reasoning sent back unless `:seon.config.ai/replay-reasoning` is false, which a config with thinking on refuses at load):
1. **The help page** ([agent](agent.md)) as the system message: the same bytes for every agent and every turn (`seon.context/help-text`).
2. **The run sections** (`seon.context/run-state`: the task and its plan, the references, the helpers it does not name, the cited sections, the evidence), made from the run's first turn's basis on the commit it read, so they stay byte-identical while the run works even when the program they cite changes.
3. **The conversation**, append-only: the run's records in tx order (```seon.context/records```: the agent's evals of the run, the user's among them, and the messages the run took), each by its own render, printed once when it is first sent and reused by its id after (```seon.context/printed-run```, kept in the agent's state per run: a record's message is a function of it and the records before it, which never change, so a turn prints only its new records and every older message is the same bytes, and the provider's cache holds; proof `seon.context-cost-test/a-turn-prints-its-new-records-flat-in-the-history`). Within `:seon.config.render/history-budget` (```seon.context/kept```) the newest records are whole, older ones collapsed (the result its handle and its value in one line, the record's `:line`; sent as `[:line id]`), the oldest left out and counted; both boundaries move in steps of `:seon.config.render/history-step` records, so the prefix before them holds for many turns; a cut never starts inside a reply.
4. **The screen**: the screen evals the turn's basis names (`:screen`), each `{:by "screen" :code .. :response ..}` by the eval fact's render, evaluated in the agent's REPL just before the request (`seon.screen/forms`: `(my.task/status)`, the turn sections of `seon.context/turn-state`, and `(my.branch/changes)`, every change the branch's merge will carry to main, from `seon.program/divergence` against the agent's `:base`), and replaced by the next request's; how many earlier turns the history budget left out. An outside agent's read, which has no screen evals, ends with the block's turn sections as one text (`seon.context/screen-message`).

A response is `{:handle :value :out :err :ex :ms :ticks :bytes}`, each key only when present (`seon.repl/response`): one per eval call, its forms run in order and stopped by the first exception, printed by the one printer under its handle, the var `e` plus the eval's id in the `result` namespace (`seon.print/response-text`), which holds it. Its record keeps the response whole, its value as data when it is plain data (```seon.db/plain?```, stored once under its content key when large), else the response as it printed then and the value's type (```seon.repl/record-details```); nothing printed for a reader is stored, so a record is the same whatever came before it. When the conversation sends it, a value or output an earlier record of the run showed prints as the form that reads it there (`(:value result/e12)`), and its ref names that fold (`[:eval id {:value result/e12}]`), so the debug page prints the same bytes from the record and the ref alone. A reply with prose and no call ends the turn, and the run waits for the user (tool_choice auto: DeepSeek refuses `required` with thinking on).

So each request starts with the whole of the one before but its screen (`seon.context-test/the-request-is-a-conversation-of-evals-then-the-screen`), and the plan, its references and its sections go out once a request (`seon.context-test/each-section-is-sent-once-per-request`). Not this: the block sent twice a request, or a note that edits an earlier message, which breaks the prefix for every later turn. v1 measured the same split live: stable run sections of about 8.5k tokens, turn sections of about 0.75k, 85-91% cache hits. Measured through the server on three open tasks (printer estimate, `:seon.config.ai/chars-per-token-prior`): the help page is 2,132 tokens, the same for every agent; the run sections 1,639-1,887 (the references 2.1-2.4 KB); the turn sections 45-76 before any proof has run. A first request is 3.9k tokens, 54% of it shared by every agent; each later turn adds its scrollback and about 60 tokens of turn sections, so with 2k tokens of scrollback a turn sends about 2.1k uncached tokens and the cached prefix is 65% of the second request and 79% of the fourth. What the provider served from its cache is recorded on each turn (`:seon.turn/cache-hit-tokens`, `:seon.turn/cache-miss-tokens`, by `seon.turn/usage`), absent when it did not say.

DeepSeek's prefix cache ([its guide](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/kv_cache)) serves a request only from a stored prefix unit it fully matches. Units are made at the end of each request's input and output, at prefixes several requests share (found lazily: the third request hits what two earlier ones shared), and at fixed intervals inside a long input; the cache is best effort, takes seconds to build, and is dropped hours to days after its last use. Since each request drops the one before's last message, the newest scrollback turn sits on no unit boundary and hits only through the shared-prefix units. Once the scrollback passes `:seon.config.render/history-budget`, the history cut (`seon.context/request`) drops the oldest kept record on every turn, so the message after the run sections changes each request and from then on the whole scrollback misses the cache.

A reply cut at `:seon.config.ai/max-tokens` (`:seon.turn/finish` "length"; `seon.model/reply-of` keeps the cut text and the forgiving reader repairs it) is almost always a repetition loop, not long work. Over 370 deepseek-flash turns with thinking off and a 65,536-token bound, 66 (18%) ended "length" at 184k-415k characters, each repeating 2-47 distinct forms (typically one ``clojure.repl/source`` call 300+ times), every form evaluated (up to 4,206 in one turn); they spent 45% of all tokens and 168 of 181 model-minutes (median 151 s against 2 s for an ordinary turn). 18 of 33 first turns looped, so the loop is not only history fed back. The largest ordinary reply was 19,809 characters (about 5k tokens).

The field's evidence for this shape: SWE-agent (arXiv 2405.15793, SWE-bench Lite, 18.0% resolved) lost 3.0 points with full history instead of collapsing all but the last 5 observations, 5.3 with a whole-file viewer instead of 100 lines, 6.0 with iterative instead of summarized search and 1.7 with no demonstration, and its successes took a median of 12 steps against a mean of 21 for its failures. CodeAct (arXiv 2402.01030) found code as the one action up to 20% more successful in fewer turns; Seon's one `eval` tool is CodeAct ([model](model.md#one-tool-eval)). aider ranks a 1k-token map of symbols by references, and Manus calls the KV-cache hit rate the first metric and keeps its context append-only and deterministically serialized.


## How a value is printed

A value is printed in two passes, selection under one token budget and then layout, so its text and its hiccup come from one selection and every cut names the form that reads the rest.

```seon.print/print-value``` is the one entry: a value, a handle the cuts read through (``result/h3``, or a form), a budget in tokens (```seon.print/budgets```: a reply's, a part's, a form's and the context's, each a `:seon.config.render*` key), a depth and a width. ```seon.print.select/show``` selects: it allots the budget breadth before depth (every child of a collection shows before any one deepens), identity keys first, and builds a print-node tree whose cut nodes carry their continuation forms; a value whose metadata holds ``:seon.print/full`` prints whole, and ``:seon.print/budget`` raises its own budget. ```seon.print.emit/emit-both``` lays the tree out as fipp does (begin, end, nest, line, text over one operation stream) and answers the text, and the hiccup the page's value panel shows when asked; fipp itself is only the layout's test oracle (`seon.print-test/the-layout-is-fipps`). Printing through fipp cost 45.4 KB and 16-17 µs per entry of a flat map, the printer 2.41 KB and 1.8 µs. Not this: Jolt's `clojure.pprint`, which would give that back. ```seon.print.select/estimate``` is the one sizer of tokens. `seon.print/text` (the printed text, or the error's message when even the first part does not fit), `seon.print/line` and `seon.print/prose` are the printer at a budget for text, a row and prose ([Prose](#prose-and-the-one-string-cutter), [A render is a value](#a-render-is-a-value-printed-once)).

## One printer

There is one value printer, `seon.print`, and nothing is cut before it. A value a person or a model reads is its schema's declared render (`seon.render`) or the printer's text; a value that is not text reaching the page's serializer (`seon.web.html/html`), a one-line row (`seon.print/line`) or prose (`seon.render.ui/prose`) is printed by the printer, never `str`. The REPL answers every value through `seon.render/printed`: a value whose producer names its schema (`seon.task/task`, `seon.problems/report`: the value's metadata ``:seon.render/schema``) reads by its declared AI render, the same text root's context and the page are made from, and ends with the form that shows the whole value as data, `(with-meta result/h3 nil)`; a map that merely holds a key (a partial pull, tx data) prints as the data it is. An evaluation's error reads by the error map's render (its message, fix, variant and where; Jolt's diagnostics are keys of the map, printed once, [errors](errors.md#an-eval-errors-diagnostics)). The page's HTML renders of the same schemas show the same facts.

`seon.render-test/one-printer-and-no-cut-before-it` reads the compiler's facts (`:form/refs` of src imported, `seon.program/live-forms`; each var's form, and each method's): every var outside `seon.print` that references `pr-str`, `prn-str`, `print-str`, `with-out-str`, `pr`, `prn`, `printf` or `clojure.pprint`'s `pprint`, `pp`, `write` or `cl-format` is named below; every var of a display namespace (`my.*`, `seon.render.*`, `seon.web*`, `seon.context`, `seon.server`, `seon.cost`, `seon.repl`, `seon.agent`, `seon.problems`) that references `subs`, `take`, `take-last` or `drop-last` is named below; and every var outside them that calls the printer with a budget (`seon.print/text`, `line`, `prose`: a cut where a value is made) is named below. An unnamed one is red, and so is a name here that no longer does it. (`butlast` is left out: destructuring's expansion references it in every function. Not covered: `str`, `print`, `println` and `format` on data, and `first` or `drop` on rows shown, which the compiler's facts cannot tell from their use on data; review reads for them.)

### Printing beside the printer: the wire

Text for the wire, storage, a digest, a file, a URL, a command line or code to evaluate is `seon.edn/encode` (EDN that reads back, whole: no print limits), and only that var prints beside the printer:

- `seon.edn/encode`: EDN for storage and the wire, whole.
- `seon.context/eval-messages`, `seon.context/request`, `seon.context/parts-of`: the EDN envelope of a user message (`{:by .. :code .. :response ..}`, `{:history {:left-out n}}`) around a response the one printer already printed: the wire, not output.
- `seon.config/resolved`: the config keys a refused combination names, in its error message.
- `my.root/first-moves!`: the code text it sends an agent to evaluate (the call to `seon.judge/first-move` on a task id): code to evaluate, not output.

An agent's namespace refers `clojure.pprint`'s `pp` and `pprint` (`seon.agent/make-ns!`), as a plain Clojure REPL does (AGENTS §4, "what would a plain REPL do"); what they write reaches the model as the evaluation's `:out`, budgeted as prose. A check's console lines are the printer's whole text (`seon.main/shown`).

### Cut where a value is made

- `seon.main/shown`: names a budget only to print whole (the value's ``:seon.print/full`` metadata).
- `seon.contract/problems`, `seon.task/file-declarations`: a refused value (a function's argument, a task file's entry) may be infinite, lazy or a host object, and travels on the wire as text, so its error keeps the printer's cut, which says what it omitted; the live value stays with the caller.

### Cuts in a display namespace that are not cuts of output

- `seon.render.ui/kname`, `seon.render.ui/dom-id`: a keyword's text without its colon.
- `seon.context/similar`: the top k of a search (`take` the config's `:seon.config.render/similar` of its ranking), and the forms that search further beside them: a search's answer, not a cut of output.
- `my.agent/file-source`, `seon.repl/form-src`: a form's own text out of its file or its reply (`subs` at a reader's offsets).
- `my.root/task-files`: a task file's owning namespace from its file name (`subs` off the `.edn`).
- `seon.render.ui/utc-offset-ms`: parsing `:seon.config.web/utc-offset`.
- `seon.web/handler`, `seon.web/view-of`, `seon.server/mcp-reply`: parsing a request's path and a tool's name.
- `seon.context/near`: the whole line a repaired delimiter is on, the printer budgets it.
- `seon.render.proc/pass`: root's context keeps the newest `:seon.config.render.list/stream` events; its events section (`seon.render.ai/section-events`) says how many older it did not keep.
- `seon.render.ui/chat`: an agent's page renders the newest parts of its conversation its tab has scrolled to (`:shown`, as `seon.render.ui/page`), the older counted at its top.
- `seon.render.proc/debug-value`: the debug page renders the newest rows its tab has scrolled to (`:shown`, as `seon.render.ui/page`), the rest counted by its list's end.
- `seon.render.ui/page`: a page renders the newest rows its tab has scrolled to (`:seon.config.render.list/history` at first, as many more each time its end scrolls into view); its end (`seon.render.ui/more`) counts the older ones, unrendered.
