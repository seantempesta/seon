No, it is actually very clear if you read it carefully. The tension is:
- It says a form RUNS only if it starts with `(` on a new line, and `;` is for prose.
- But in normal chat, prose with a parenthetical at the start of a line is completely normal.

3. **What wording change would stop it?**

For (a) result glyphs:
> **DON'T MIMIC EXAMPLE OUTPUT.** The strings `⟹`, `⟸`, `=>` followed by a value, and `my.ns=>` are FORBIDDEN in your output. The runtime injects them. If you type them, your line will be deleted and your action will fail. To report a value, use only plain English in `; comments` or `message/user`, e.g. `; I saw 59.5 in result/clC` - never with arrows.

For (b) parenthetical prose:
> **CRITICAL SYNTAX RULE: ANY line that starts with `(` (after optional whitespace) is EXECUTED as code.** Human prose must NEVER start a line with `(`. Always prefix prose with `; ` or ensure it is inside a string arg to `message/user`. If you want to write "(foo)" in English, write `; (foo)` or `; Note: foo is...`. Will crash otherwise.

Making the forbidden pattern concrete with examples of what NOT to do is more effective than the abstract "those glyphs are the runtime's alone".
